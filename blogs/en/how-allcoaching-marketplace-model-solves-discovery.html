Section 01

The real problem is
discovery, not tools.

Building a course, a website and an app is now a few clicks on platforms like AllCoaching. The harder problem is being found: a new academy has no audience, and students cannot choose a teacher they have never heard of.

· · ·

Section 02

Why a standalone app struggles
— the empty shop.

A standalone app holds your content well but brings no visitors. Students have to download it, which they will only do if they already trust you. That is why most educators’ first students come from YouTube, WhatsApp, Instagram and offline batches.

· · ·

Section 03

How a marketplace listing helps
— the mechanism.

A marketplace is a place where students already browse many educators. When a student searches for a subject, exam and language, listings that match can appear. That gives a new educator a chance to be seen by students who did not know them. How often that happens depends on the niche, the course page, reviews and price.

Section 05

Own academy vs app-only
vs marketplace-only.

  • App-only: full brand, no built-in visitors.
  • Marketplace-only: visitors, but on the marketplace’s terms and brand.
  • Own academy + listing: your brand and data, plus an extra door. Students still mostly come from your own channels.

Section 06

The economics —
honestly.

Paid ads cost money for every student; organic channels cost time. A marketplace listing adds a free extra channel on top of your plan, but it does not replace marketing. Section 07

What the marketplace model is NOT —
three honest concessions.

  • Not a guarantee. No listing can promise students, sales or income.
  • Not a replacement for your channels. YouTube, WhatsApp, Google and referrals stay the main sources.
  • Not instant. Reviews and reputation take time.

fits you.

  • You want your own brand and data.
  • You will keep publishing content on your own channels.
