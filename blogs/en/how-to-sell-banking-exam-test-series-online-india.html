Section 01

A test series is a product,
not a PDF dump.

Banking aspirants already have more free questions than they can solve. What they pay for is structure: full-length mocks in the current pattern, sectional tests for weak areas, clear solutions, timed practice that feels like the exam, and a schedule that runs until exam day. Treat your series as a product with a launch date, a syllabus and a promise you can keep.

· · ·

Section 02

Six layers of a
banking test series.

  1. Exam pattern: sections, timing and marking from the latest official notification.
  2. Question bank: original questions, tagged by section and topic.
  3. Test formats: full mocks, sectional tests and topic tests.
  4. Solutions: short, clear explanations for every question.
  5. Analysis: score, accuracy and time per section so aspirants know what to fix.
  6. Schedule: a published release plan up to the exam date.
· · ·

Section 03

Which banking exams
to cover first.

Start where demand is high and the pattern is stable: IBPS PO and SBI PO prelims, IBPS Clerk and SBI Clerk prelims. Add mains, IBPS RRB (Officer Scale I and Office Assistant), RBI Assistant and RBI Grade B once your first series has paying students. Always check the latest notification before you publish; patterns change.

· · ·

Section 04

Building the
question bank.

Write questions from the current pattern and previous-year papers, in the difficulty mix the exam actually uses. AI writing tools can speed up drafting, but they make mistakes in reasoning and quant; check every question and solution yourself before it goes live. Never copy questions from another publisher’s paid series. A realistic first release is 10 to 15 full mocks plus sectional tests, with more added on a published schedule.

· · ·

Section 05

Pricing
your series.

A simple three-tier structure works for most educators: a free sample mock so aspirants can judge quality, a paid series for one exam, and a full-year pass across exams. Compare what established test-series sellers charge, then price on the quality and recency you can deliver. These are your prices; the platform plan is a separate cost.

· · ·

Section 06

Reaching aspirants
without paid ads.

  • YouTube: short solution videos and strategy sessions that link to your free mock.
  • Telegram and WhatsApp: a daily question or quiz in a community you run, linking to the paid series.
  • Marketplace listing: on AllCoaching your courses can also be listed on the AllCoaching marketplace app. It is an extra channel; it does not guarantee students.

None of these is instant. Plan for months of consistent posting before the series sells steadily.

· · ·

Section 07

Start now
or wait?

Start now if you can write and check questions in the current pattern, have time to publish on a fixed schedule, and already reach some aspirants (a channel, a group, past students). Wait if you have no audience at all: build one first with free content, then launch the paid series.
