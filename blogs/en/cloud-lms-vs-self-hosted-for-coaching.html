    What self-hosted and cloud LMS actually mean

    A self-hosted LMS is learning software you install on a server you rent, and you are responsible for keeping it running. A cloud LMS is learning software the vendor runs for you; you log in through a browser and your students use the website and app.

    Self-hosted: you own the server and the work

    The most common self-hosted setup for a small academy is WordPress plus an LMS plugin. Tutor LMS and LearnDash are two of the best known. Larger institutions sometimes run Moodle or Open edX, which are open-source LMS platforms built for universities and big training teams. In every case you pick the hosting, install the software, and keep it secure and updated.

    That brings real freedom. You can edit any code, add any plugin, pick any theme and move hosts when you like. Nobody can change your pricing or switch off a feature you depend on.

    Cloud: the platform runs it, you teach

    With a cloud LMS, the vendor runs the servers, applies updates, handles security and keeps the checkout working. You build your courses, set prices and teach. The trade is that you work inside the features the platform offers, and you pay a plan price, sometimes with a fee on sales.

    Question educators ask

    Is self-hosting the same as owning my academy?

    Not quite. Self-hosting means you own the server and the code. Owning your academy means students know your brand, your domain and your app, and you can export your student data. A good cloud LMS gives you the second without the first.

    The jobs that move to you when you self-host

    Here is the honest list. None of these is hard on its own. Together they become a second job.

    • Hosting: choosing a plan with enough capacity for exam-day logins and renewing it every year.
    • Updates: WordPress core, the theme, the LMS plugin and every add-on, tested so one update does not break another.
    • Security: SSL, logins, backups, malware checks and restoring the site if something goes wrong.
    • Payments: opening a gateway account such as Razorpay, connecting it to the plugin and testing UPI and card checkout.
    • Video: hosting lectures somewhere that can handle hundreds of students at once.
    • Mobile app: a separate project if you want one in your name.

    What a cloud LMS and a self-hosted LMS really cost

    A self-hosted LMS costs the plugin price plus hosting, a domain, payment gateway fees, any paid add-ons and the time of whoever keeps it running. A cloud LMS costs its plan price plus any fee on sales. The second is easier to predict because it is all on one pricing page.

    The self-hosted bill, line by line

    • LMS plugin: Tutor LMS core is free and Pro starts at $199 a year; LearnDash starts at $350 for one site.
    • Hosting: quoted by your hosting company, and it should be sized for exam-day traffic, not a quiet Tuesday.
    • Domain and SSL: yearly renewals.
    • Payment gateway: your gateway's own fee on each payment.
    • Video hosting: a separate service if you do not want lectures on your own server.
    • Developer time: for setup, updates and the evening a plugin update breaks checkout.
    • Mobile app: a separate build if you want one. Our guide to white-label coaching app development cost explains the options.

    We have not put rupee figures on hosting or developer time, because they vary by provider and city and nobody publishes a standard price. Ask for written quotes before you decide.

    Which one fits your coaching business

    Solo tutor or new coaching (no developer)

    Choose a cloud LMS. Your scarcest resource is time, and every hour spent on plugin updates is an hour not spent on lectures or marketing. Our guide to starting an online coaching academy without coding walks through the first week.

    Growing institute with a few teachers

    A cloud LMS still usually wins, because you need separate teacher logins, reliable live classes and a checkout that works on exam-result day. Make sure the platform lets you connect your own domain; see LMS with a custom domain for coaching.

    You already run a WordPress site with steady traffic

    Adding Tutor LMS or LearnDash to that site can make sense, especially if a developer already maintains it. Plan for payments early; our guide to the best UPI payment gateway for online courses covers the options.

    University, large training company or strict data rules

    Self-hosting Moodle or Open edX is reasonable when an IT team already runs servers and policy requires data in-house. The cost is a team, not a plugin.

    Video-heavy courses

    Whichever way you go, decide where lectures will live. Read secure video hosting for educational content before you upload your first batch.

    How to move from a self-hosted LMS to a cloud LMS

    The safe way to move is to build the new academy first and run both side by side. Current students finish on the old site; new admissions go to the new one.

    1. Build your academy on the cloud LMSStart the 7-day trial, connect your domain or a subdomain, and upload one complete course with its demo lesson.
    2. Run both side by sideKeep current students on your self-hosted site until their batch or validity ends, so nobody loses access.
    3. Point new admissions to the new academyChange the buy buttons on your old site, YouTube and WhatsApp to your new academy and app links.
    4. Export records and retire the old hostingDownload student and payment records from WordPress, keep a backup, then let the hosting and plugin licence lapse at renewal.

    When educators ask us whether to install WordPress or sign up for a cloud LMS, our first question is always the same: who will fix the site at 11 pm before a mock test? If the answer is you, start with the cloud and keep your evenings for teaching. The full checklist, including messages to send students, is in how to switch coaching apps without losing students.

    Glossary

    Cloud LMS
    A learning management system the vendor hosts and maintains; you and your students use it through a browser or app.
    Self-hosted LMS
    A learning management system you install on a server you rent or own, and keep updated and secure yourself.
    LMS plugin
    An add-on such as Tutor LMS or LearnDash that turns a WordPress website into a course platform.
    Payment gateway
    A service such as Razorpay that accepts UPI and card payments on your site and settles them to your bank account.
    Custom domain
    Your own web address, such as youracademy.in, connected to your academy instead of a platform subdomain.
    Platform fee
    A percentage a cloud platform keeps from each sale. AllCoaching's is 0% on every monthly plan, and 10%, 7.5% or 5% on the yearly Starter, Growth or Pro plan.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is a self-hosted LMS cheaper than a cloud LMS for coaching?

    Only in software. Tutor LMS has a free core and Pro from $199 a year, but you also pay for hosting, domain, SSL, payment gateway fees and the time to keep it running. A cloud LMS such as AllCoaching includes those jobs, with 0% commission on every monthly plan from ₹3,999 a month, or yearly from ₹6,999 plus 10% of sales.

    What is the biggest hidden cost of a self-hosted LMS?

    Time. Someone has to update WordPress, the theme and every plugin, keep backups, fix security issues and keep payments working. If that person is you, it comes out of teaching hours; if it is a developer, it is a recurring bill.

    Which is more secure, a cloud LMS or a self-hosted LMS?

    Neither is secure by default. A self-hosted LMS is as secure as the person maintaining it. A cloud LMS puts that job on the vendor, so ask how it handles updates, backups and data export before you sign up.

    Is AllCoaching cloud or self-hosted?

    AllCoaching is a cloud LMS. It runs the hosting, updates, payments, student access and your own Android app (iOS app on Pro or any yearly plan), while you build and brand the academy yourself on your own domain.

    When is a self-hosted LMS the right choice for a coaching business?

    When you already have a developer or IT team, already run a WordPress site with traffic, need custom code, or must keep data on your own servers. For most solo tutors and small institutes, a cloud LMS saves more time than it costs.

    What do Tutor LMS and LearnDash cost?

    Tutor LMS has a free core plugin and Pro plans from $199 a year for one site, or $499 for a lifetime licence, excluding taxes. LearnDash is sold as a bundle from $350 for one site, and its page does not say whether that is yearly. Both prices are in US dollars, checked 24 Oct 2026.

    Does a cloud LMS lock me in?

    It can if you cannot export your data. Before you choose any cloud LMS, check that you can download student lists and payment records and connect your own domain, so students follow your brand if you ever move.

    Can I move from WordPress to a cloud LMS without losing students?

    Yes. Build the new academy, keep current students on WordPress until their batch ends, send new admissions to the new academy, then export records and let the old hosting lapse.
