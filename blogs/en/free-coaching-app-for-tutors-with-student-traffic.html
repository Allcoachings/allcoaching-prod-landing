Section 01

The "free coaching app" misconception —
hosting is the easy part.

Tutors searching for a "free coaching app with student traffic" usually want two things: somewhere to host courses without a big bill, and students who arrive without paid ads. The first is easy to find in 2026. The second is not something any app can promise.

Hosting, checkout and an app are the solved part. Platforms such as AllCoaching let you build them yourself in a few clicks, without a developer. The hard part is being found: a new academy starts with no audience, and students come from the places where they already know you.

Section 02

What "student traffic" actually means —
and where it comes from.

Student traffic is people who could buy your course arriving at your academy. It is not the same as page visits: a visit only matters if the person is looking for what you teach, in the language you teach it, at a price they can pay.

  • Your existing audience — past students, parents, WhatsApp groups, your offline batch. Usually the first paying students.
  • YouTube and Instagram — free lessons build trust; the link in the description sends viewers to your academy.
  • Google search — pages on your own domain that answer a specific search can rank over time; it is slow work.
  • Marketplace listings — places where students already browse, such as the AllCoaching marketplace app. An extra source, rarely the main one.
  • Paid ads — optional; test them once you know which course converts.

Section 03

How a marketplace listing helps —
and its limits.

On AllCoaching, every plan lists your courses on the AllCoaching marketplace app on Android, iOS and web. Students can search by subject, exam and language and find your academy there, alongside other educators.

What helps a listing: a specific niche ("NEET Biology, Hindi medium"), a clear course page with price and outcome, a free demo lesson, and honest reviews from real students. What it cannot do: guarantee visits, enrolments or income. Treat it as one more door next to your own channels.

Students find educators, not platforms. The platform decides how easy it is to find you, trust you and pay.
