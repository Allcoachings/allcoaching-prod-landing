Section 01

The discovery surface changed —
from ranking to citation.

Students increasingly ask an AI assistant "best online physics teacher for NEET in Hindi" instead of scrolling ten blue links. The answer quotes a few sources, often without a click. Classic SEO still matters, because many AI answers draw on pages that already rank, but being quoted needs pages that are easy to parse, clearly attributed and directly answer the question.

· · ·

Section 02

Six layers of an
AI-readable coaching website.

  1. Structured data: schema.org JSON-LD for Organization, Person, Course, FAQPage, Article and BreadcrumbList.
  2. Entity signals: the same name, logo and description everywhere, with sameAs links to your YouTube, Instagram and other profiles.
  3. Direct-answer content: the first sentence of each key page answers the question; add short summaries and FAQs.
  4. Crawler access and llms.txt: allow the AI crawlers in robots.txt and offer an llms.txt index of your key pages.
  5. A named author: real credentials and experience, with a Person schema.
  6. Freshness: accurate dateModified and regular updates to fees, batches and syllabus.
· · ·

Section 03

SEO-only site vs optimized site
vs listings.

  • SEO-only site: can rank, but often lacks clear answers, structured data and author signals.
  • Optimized site: all six layers; still needs time to earn recognition on a new domain.
  • Listings elsewhere (YouTube, directories, marketplaces): add mentions and another way to be found; they complement your own site rather than replace it.

Most educators benefit from an optimized site of their own plus consistent profiles elsewhere.

· · ·

Section 04

What the evidence
says.

Research on generative engine optimization (for example the 2023 Princeton-led GEO study) found that adding citations, quotations and statistics to content raised its visibility in AI-generated answers in their tests. Google’s own guidance for AI features says the same things that help regular search apply: helpful, reliable, people-first content, crawlable pages and structured data that matches the visible text. Results vary by query and engine; treat any single number as indicative, not a promise.

· · ·

Section 05

Why distribution
still matters.

AI engines tend to cite sources that are already recognised: mentioned on other sites, linked, reviewed and consistent. A brand-new domain starts with little of that. Build recognition alongside the technical work: publish useful content on YouTube and your blog, collect genuine student reviews, get listed where students already search, and keep your name and details identical everywhere.

· · ·

Section 06

What GEO
is not.

  • Not a guarantee. No one controls which sources an AI engine quotes.
  • Not a one-time setup. Fees, batches and dates change; stale pages lose trust.
  • Not a replacement for teaching quality. Reviews and results still decide whether students enrol.

Section 08

A 14-day
playbook.

  1. Days 1–3: add Organization, Person, Course and FAQPage structured data; validate it.
  2. Days 4–5: update robots.txt to allow GPTBot, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and Google-Extended; publish an llms.txt.
  3. Days 6–9: rewrite your top five pages so the first sentence answers the main question; add FAQs that match the schema word for word.
  4. Days 10–12: add a named author bio with credentials and sameAs links; make your name and details consistent across profiles.
  5. Days 13–14: set dateModified correctly, then plan monthly updates and a steady flow of genuine reviews and mentions.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What does it mean to optimize a coaching website for AI agents?

It means structuring your content, data and identity so AI engines such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude and Google AI Overviews can parse it, trust it and quote it. This is called generative engine optimization (GEO).

What is the difference between SEO and GEO for a coaching website?

SEO aims to rank a link that a person clicks. GEO aims to be the source an AI quotes in its answer. They overlap: many AI answers draw on pages that already rank well.

What structured data should a coaching website add for AI agents?

Organization, Person (for the teacher), Course, FAQPage, Article and BreadcrumbList, all matching the visible text on the page.

Does adding schema markup guarantee my coaching site gets cited by AI?

No. Structured data makes your pages easier to read, but no one controls which sources an AI engine quotes.

What is llms.txt and does a coaching website need one?

It is a plain-text index at your site root listing your key pages for AI tools. It is cheap to add and can help, but it is not a ranking guarantee.

Should I block or allow AI crawlers like GPTBot on my coaching website?

If you want to be cited by AI products, allow the crawlers they use, such as GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and Google-Extended, in robots.txt.

How do AI agents decide which coaching website to cite?

They weigh whether a page can be parsed, whether the source is recognised, whether it directly answers the question, whether it looks credible and whether it is current.

Should I build my own website or rely on a marketplace for AI discovery?

Build your own website and domain first, so your content builds your brand, and use marketplaces and other profiles as extra channels. On AllCoaching you get both: your own website and a listing on the AllCoaching marketplace app.

How long does it take to make a coaching website AI-citable?

The technical layers take about two weeks for a small site. Recognition, through mentions, reviews and links, builds over months.

Will optimizing for AI agents hurt my normal Google SEO?

No. The same practices, clear answers, structured data, a named author and fresh content, also support regular search.
