    <section id="why-switch">
      <h2>Where organic students actually come from</h2>
      <p>Organic students reach an educator through four channels. A platform cannot replace any of them, but it can make each one work better or worse.</p>
      <h3>1. Google search on your own domain</h3>
      <p>Students type exact needs into Google: "SSC CGL maths course in Hindi", "spoken English classes online for housewives", "NEET biology test series". A page on your own domain that answers that search can rank for years. It is slow work. Google's own guide is plain about the timing:<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup></p>
      <div class="v3-quote"><p>"Some changes might take effect in a few hours, others could take several months."</p><cite>Google Search Central, SEO Starter Guide</cite></div>
      <p>It also does not happen by default: an Ahrefs study found that 96.55% of content gets no traffic from Google.<sup><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup> Pages that rank are written for a specific search. Our <a href="/blog/seo-for-online-academy-beginners-guide">SEO for an online academy</a> guide shows how.</p>
      <h3>2. YouTube</h3>
      <p>For most Indian educators, YouTube is the largest free channel. Free lessons build trust; the link in the description turns viewers into students. What matters here is that the link opens your own academy, on your own name, with a clear course and price.</p>
      <h3>3. Instagram, WhatsApp and Telegram</h3>
      <p>These channels carry the people who already know you. A yoga teacher's reels or a banking educator's WhatsApp group can fill a batch, if the payment step is one tap and works with UPI.</p>
      <h3>4. Marketplaces and listings</h3>
      <p>A marketplace is a place where students already browse courses. It can add students you would not have reached, but it works on the marketplace's terms, and it is rarely the main source for an independent educator.</p>
      <div class="v3-q">
        <p class="l">Question educators ask</p>
        <h3>Does Graphy bring organic traffic to my courses?</h3>
        <p>Graphy's pricing page describes a website builder, branded apps, digital products and AI sales and support agents. It does not describe a built-in student marketplace. Your Graphy site can still rank on Google and receive YouTube traffic, so ask Graphy directly what discovery features your plan includes.</p>

      <dl>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-organic-traffic">Organic traffic</dfn></dt>
        <dd>Visitors who reach your academy without a paid ad, for example from Google search, YouTube, social posts or a marketplace listing.</dd>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-marketplace-listing">Marketplace listing</dfn></dt>
        <dd>A page for your course inside a marketplace app or site where students already browse, in addition to your own academy.</dd>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-custom-domain">Custom domain</dfn></dt>
        <dd>Your own web address, such as youracademy.in, connected to your academy website; search visibility you build there stays with you.</dd>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-seo">SEO</dfn></dt>
        <dd>Search engine optimisation: making pages that answer what students search, so Google can show them.</dd>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-meta-pixel">Meta Pixel</dfn></dt>
        <dd>A small code from Meta that measures visits and purchases from Facebook and Instagram, so you can see which posts and ads bring students.</dd>
