    <section id="what-it-means">
      <h2>What self-hosted and cloud LMS actually mean</h2>
      <p>A self-hosted LMS is learning software you install on a server you rent, and you are responsible for keeping it running. A cloud LMS is learning software the vendor runs for you; you log in through a browser and your students use the website and app.</p>
      <h3>Self-hosted: you own the server and the work</h3>
      <p>The most common self-hosted setup for a small academy is WordPress plus an LMS plugin. Tutor LMS and LearnDash are two of the best known. Larger institutions sometimes run Moodle or Open edX, which are open-source LMS platforms built for universities and big training teams. In every case you pick the hosting, install the software, and keep it secure and updated.</p>
      <p>That brings real freedom. You can edit any code, add any plugin, pick any theme and move hosts when you like. Nobody can change your pricing or switch off a feature you depend on.</p>
      <h3>Cloud: the platform runs it, you teach</h3>
      <p>With a cloud LMS, the vendor runs the servers, applies updates, handles security and keeps the checkout working. You build your courses, set prices and teach. The trade is that you work inside the features the platform offers, and you pay a plan price, sometimes with a fee on sales.</p>
      <div class="v3-q">
        <p class="l">Question educators ask</p>
        <h3>Is self-hosting the same as owning my academy?</h3>
        <p>Not quite. Self-hosting means you own the server and the code. Owning your academy means students know your brand, your domain and your app, and you can export your student data. A good cloud LMS gives you the second without the first.</p>
      </div>
      <h3>The jobs that move to you when you self-host</h3>
      <p>Here is the honest list. None of these is hard on its own. Together they become a second job.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Hosting</strong>: choosing a plan with enough capacity for exam-day logins and renewing it every year.</li>
        <li><strong>Updates</strong>: WordPress core, the theme, the LMS plugin and every add-on, tested so one update does not break another.</li>
        <li><strong>Security</strong>: SSL, logins, backups, malware checks and restoring the site if something goes wrong.</li>
        <li><strong>Payments</strong>: opening a gateway account such as Razorpay, connecting it to the plugin and testing UPI and card checkout.</li>
        <li><strong>Video</strong>: hosting lectures somewhere that can handle hundreds of students at once.</li>
        <li><strong>Mobile app</strong>: a separate project if you want one in your name.</li>
      </ul>

      <h2>Which one fits your coaching business</h2>
      <h3>Solo tutor or new coaching (no developer)</h3>
      <p>Choose a cloud LMS. Your scarcest resource is time, and every hour spent on plugin updates is an hour not spent on lectures or marketing. Our guide to starting an <a href="/blog/online-coaching-academy-without-coding">online coaching academy without coding</a> walks through the first week.</p>
      <h3>Growing institute with a few teachers</h3>
      <p>A cloud LMS still usually wins, because you need separate teacher logins, reliable live classes and a checkout that works on exam-result day. Make sure the platform lets you connect your own domain; see <a href="/blog/lms-with-custom-domain-for-coaching-india">LMS with a custom domain for coaching</a>.</p>
      <h3>You already run a WordPress site with steady traffic</h3>
      <p>Adding Tutor LMS or LearnDash to that site can make sense, especially if a developer already maintains it. Plan for payments early; our guide to the <a href="/blog/best-upi-payment-gateway-for-online-courses">best UPI payment gateway for online courses</a> covers the options.</p>
      <h3>University, large training company or strict data rules</h3>
      <p>Self-hosting Moodle or Open edX is reasonable when an IT team already runs servers and policy requires data in-house. The cost is a team, not a plugin.</p>

      <h2>Glossary</h2>
      <dl>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-cloud-lms">Cloud LMS</dfn></dt>
        <dd>A learning management system the vendor hosts and maintains; you and your students use it through a browser or app.</dd>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-self-hosted-lms">Self-hosted LMS</dfn></dt>
        <dd>A learning management system you install on a server you rent or own, and keep updated and secure yourself.</dd>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-lms-plugin">LMS plugin</dfn></dt>
        <dd>An add-on such as Tutor LMS or LearnDash that turns a WordPress website into a course platform.</dd>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-payment-gateway">Payment gateway</dfn></dt>
        <dd>A service such as Razorpay that accepts UPI and card payments on your site and settles them to your bank account.</dd>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-custom-domain">Custom domain</dfn></dt>
        <dd>Your own web address, such as youracademy.in, connected to your academy instead of a platform subdomain.</dd>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-platform-fee">Platform fee</dfn></dt>
        <dd>A percentage a cloud platform keeps from each sale. AllCoaching's is 0% on every monthly plan, and 10%, 7.5% or 5% on the yearly Starter, Growth or Pro plan.</dd>
      </dl>
