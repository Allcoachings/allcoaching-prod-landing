      <p><strong>Educators usually leave subscription platforms because a fixed fee is paid before any student has paid them, and in a slow year that fee eats a large share of what they earn.</strong> Pay-as-you-sell pricing, where most of the cost is a percentage of sales, moves the cost to when money is actually coming in. But it is not always cheaper: once your sales are high, a fixed fee can cost less.</p>
      <p>So the real question is not "subscription or not". It is which pricing model fits your sales today and next year. This guide compares the two models with prices from each platform's own website, checked on 24 Oct 2026, and computes where each one wins.</p>

    <section id="why-switch">
      <h2>Why educators leave subscription platforms</h2>
      <p>Subscription platforms are not bad products. Most do what they promise: host courses, run live classes, take payments and give you an app. The reasons educators move are usually about how the price fits their income.</p>
      <h3>1. The fee comes before the first sale</h3>
      <p>A fixed yearly fee is paid in full at the start. For a new SSC maths tutor or a yoga teacher building a first batch, that can be most of the first year's income. A model that charges mostly on sales means a slow month costs little.</p>
      <h3>2. Teaching income is seasonal</h3>
      <p>Exam educators sell most courses in the months before an exam. A fixed fee is the same in a busy month and in a quiet one, so in a quiet year it costs more per rupee earned.</p>
      <h3>3. The total is hard to see upfront</h3>
      <p>Some pricing pages leave out GST, any fee on sales, the renewal price or the cost of the app. None of that is wrong on its own, but it makes the true yearly cost hard to compare. Ask for each one in writing before you renew.</p>
      <div class="v3-q">
        <p class="l">Question educators ask</p>
        <h3>Is the subscription model the problem, or my platform?</h3>
        <p>Usually neither on its own. A fixed fee is a good deal at high, steady sales and a poor one at low or uneven sales. If your sales have changed since you signed up, the model that fits you may have changed too.</p>
      </div>
      <h3>4. Wanting to own and change things yourself</h3>
      <p>Many educators now want their own domain, their own app and the ability to change a price or add a course in minutes, without a request to the vendor. That pushes them toward self-serve academy platforms, whatever the pricing model.</p>

      <h2>Glossary</h2>
      <dl>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-subscription-platform">Subscription platform</dfn></dt>
        <dd>A course or coaching platform that charges a fixed monthly or yearly fee, whether or not you make sales.</dd>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-pay-as-you-sell">Pay-as-you-sell</dfn></dt>
        <dd>A pricing model where most of the platform's charge is a percentage of each sale.</dd>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-platform-fee">Platform fee</dfn></dt>
        <dd>A percentage a platform keeps from each sale. AllCoaching's is 0% on every monthly plan, and 10%, 7.5% or 5% on the yearly Starter, Growth or Pro plan.</dd>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-breakeven">Breakeven point</dfn></dt>
        <dd>The sales level at which two pricing plans cost the same; below or above it, one is cheaper.</dd>
        <dt><dfn id="dfn-renewal-price">Renewal price</dfn></dt>
        <dd>What a plan costs when it renews after the first term, which can differ from the first-year price.</dd>
      </dl>

      <h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
      <details open><summary>Why are educators leaving subscription platforms?</summary><p>Mostly because a fixed fee is paid before any sales, and in a slow or seasonal year it takes a large share of income. Some also want their own domain and app, or a price page that shows GST, fees and renewal in full. Subscription platforms still suit educators with high, steady sales.</p></details>
      <details open><summary>What does a subscription course platform cost in India?</summary><p>Published prices vary. Classplus lists ₹29,999 for 1 year and ₹39,999 for 2 years. Edmingle starts at ₹7,499 a month billed annually and Learnyst Professional at ₹8,099 a month billed annually, both plus taxes. Teachmint does not list prices. All checked 24 Oct 2026.</p></details>
      <details open><summary>What is the difference between a subscription platform and pay-as-you-sell?</summary><p>A subscription platform charges a fixed fee whether you sell or not. A pay-as-you-sell platform charges mainly a percentage of each sale, so you pay more when you earn more and little when you do not.</p></details>
      <details open><summary>Is pay-as-you-sell always cheaper than a subscription?</summary><p>No. At low sales it is usually cheaper; at high sales a fixed fee can cost less. On published prices, AllCoaching costs less than Classplus up to about ₹2.3 lakh of sales a year, and Classplus is lower on paper above that.</p></details>
      <details open><summary>Is AllCoaching a subscription platform?</summary><p>Partly. AllCoaching's monthly plans (₹3,999, ₹5,999 or ₹7,999 a month) take 0% commission with no lock-in, and yearly Starter is ₹6,999 a year plus a 10% fee on sales. GST is extra and there is a 7-day free trial. It is not a free tier: on a yearly plan most of the cost moves with your sales, and on a monthly plan the cost is fixed and you keep 100% of each sale.</p></details>
      <details open><summary>What should I do if I am locked into a yearly subscription?</summary><p>Use the time. Build your new academy during the 7-day trial, keep current students where they are, send new admissions to your own website and app, and let the old plan end at renewal. Ask your vendor in writing about notice periods and data export.</p></details>
      <details open><summary>Will my students follow me if I switch platforms?</summary><p>They follow your brand more than the platform. If your academy uses your own domain and app, and you tell students early, current batches finish where they are and new students join you on the new academy.</p></details>
