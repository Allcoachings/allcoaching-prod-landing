    <section id="three-paths">
      <h2>The three paths, explained in plain words</h2>
      <p>There are three real choices, not two: build your own academy, teach on someone else's platform, or build your own and also list on a marketplace.</p>
      <h3>Path 1: Build your own academy and app</h3>
      <p>You get a website on your own domain, such as youracademy.in, and an Android and iOS app in your name. Students buy from you, and your student list is yours. You can do this in two ways: hire an agency or app builder to set it up for you, or set it up yourself on an academy platform.</p>
      <p>The catch is that students do not arrive on their own. You bring them from YouTube, Instagram, WhatsApp, Telegram, Google search or your offline batches.</p>
      <h3>Path 2: Join a marketplace or teach for a big platform</h3>
      <p>Here you teach inside someone else's brand. On a course marketplace such as Udemy, you publish a course to an existing learner base. With Indian edtech brands such as Vedantu, Testbook, Physics Wallah or Unacademy, you usually apply to teach for them, and they handle marketing and technology.</p>
      <p>The benefit is reach without marketing. The cost is control: the platform sets the terms, the price and the brand students remember.</p>
      <div class="v3-q">
        <p class="l">Question educators ask</p>
        <h3>If a marketplace brings students, why build my own academy at all?</h3>
        <p>Because the students belong to the platform's brand, not yours. If the platform changes its terms or its ranking, your income changes with it. Your own academy is the one place where the student relationship, the price and the brand stay with you.</p>
      </div>
      <h3>Path 3: Your own academy, plus marketplace discovery</h3>
      <p>This is the Shopify model applied to coaching. Your academy is your home: your domain, your app, your prices. A marketplace listing is one more doorway into it. On AllCoaching, your courses can also be discovered on the AllCoaching app, while your own website and app stay the main place you sell.</p>

    <section id="marketplace-alone">
      <h2>When a marketplace alone is the right choice</h2>
      <p>A marketplace or big platform on its own is the right choice when you want to teach and earn without building an audience, at least for now. That is a sensible decision, not a failure.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>You are new to teaching online</strong> and want to learn how students respond before investing time in your own academy.</li>
        <li><strong>You have no audience yet</strong> on YouTube, Instagram or WhatsApp, and do not want to do marketing.</li>
        <li><strong>You want predictable hours and income</strong>, for example teaching evening classes for a platform while keeping a school job.</li>
        <li><strong>You are testing a topic</strong>, such as a short Excel or spoken English course, with a wide audience.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>The time to start your own academy is when students begin to ask for you by name, when you have a few hundred followers who trust you, or when the platform's terms start to limit how you teach or price.</p>
      <h3>When an agency-built custom app is the right choice</h3>
      <p>A fully custom app makes sense for a large institute with a real budget, a tech person on staff and features no platform offers. For everyone else, setting up your own academy on a platform gives you the same brand ownership in days, and you can change it yourself.</p>
    </section>
