<p><strong>Key Takeaways</strong> — how AI search will change student-teacher discovery in six facts:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Students now ask AI assistants</strong> which course or teacher to choose, and get one synthesised answer with a few cited sources.</li>
<li><strong>AI search cites pages it can read and trust:</strong> crawlable pages, clear answers, structured data, a recognisable author and consistent mentions elsewhere.</li>
<li><strong>Content behind an app download wall is invisible</strong> to AI search; a website on your own domain is not.</li>
<li><strong>No platform can guarantee AI citations or rankings.</strong> You can only make your pages easier to read, trust and cite.</li>
<li><strong>Start with one page per course</strong> that answers one specific search: exam, subject, language, price and outcome.</li>
<li><strong>On AllCoaching</strong> your academy runs on your own domain with a page builder and blog, and every plan includes a listing on the AllCoaching marketplace app as an extra channel.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="h-chap font-display mt-3">Three eras of discovery —<br/><em>from links to answers.</em></h2>
<p class="mt-7 drop-cap">First, students found teachers by word of mouth and local reputation. Then by search results and paid ads. Now, increasingly, by asking an AI assistant: "best Hindi-medium NEET Biology course online". The answer names a few options and cites a few sources. Being one of those sources is the new visibility.</p>

<section class="py-14 md:py-16 border-b border-[#E6E8F0]" id="ch2">
<p class="kicker">Section 02</p>
<h2 class="h-chap font-display mt-3">How AI search picks sources —<br/><em>four things that matter.</em></h2>
<ol class="mt-7"><li><strong>Can it read the page?</strong> Public HTML, not content locked inside an app.</li><li><strong>Does the page answer the question directly?</strong> Clear headings, short answers, FAQs.</li><li><strong>Is the source recognisable?</strong> A named educator, consistent profiles, mentions on other sites.</li><li><strong>Is it structured?</strong> Schema markup for courses, FAQs and the author.</li></ol><p>Google’s own guidance is that AI features use the same signals as search: helpful, reliable, people-first content.</p>
</section>

      <div class="mt-8 space-y-4">

        <details>
          <summary>How will AI search change how students find teachers?</summary>
          <p>Students increasingly ask AI assistants which course or teacher to choose and read one synthesised answer with a few cited sources. Being a cited source matters more than ranking tenth on a results page.</p>
        </details>

        <details>
          <summary>How does AI search choose which educators to cite?</summary>
          <p>It favours pages it can read and trust: public pages that answer the question directly, a recognisable author, consistent mentions elsewhere, and structured data. Nobody can guarantee a citation.</p>
        </details>

        <details>
          <summary>Can content inside a coaching app be found by AI search?</summary>
          <p>Usually not. Content behind an app download is hard to crawl. A website on your own domain with public course pages is easier for AI search to read.</p>
        </details>

        <details>
          <summary>Does a big EdTech platform help my AI visibility?</summary>
          <p>AI search tends to cite the platform brand rather than the individual teacher, so your own reputation builds slowly there.</p>
        </details>

        <details>
          <summary>What should an educator do first?</summary>
          <p>Publish one clear course page per exam and language on your own domain, add an author page and FAQs, and keep your profiles consistent. Then check what AI assistants cite.</p>
        </details>
