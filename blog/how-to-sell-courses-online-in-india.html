    How to sell courses online in India: the eight steps

    Selling your first online course in India takes eight steps: pick the course, validate demand, record a minimum course, set the price, build the course page, turn on UPI checkout, launch to your warm audience, and use the first buyers to grow the next batch. Done in order, many educators can get through them in four to six weeks (illustrative).

    1. Pick a course people already ask forChoose one exam or skill topic students repeatedly ask you about and write it as one outcome, for one buyer, in one language.
    2. Validate demand for freeLook for the same request in YouTube comments, a WhatsApp group poll, Google Trends and search suggestions, then ask five students if they would pay.
    3. Record a minimum courseOutline and record a free demo lesson, 8 to 12 focused video lessons, PDF notes and a short test that fully deliver the promise.
    4. Set the pricePrice against the result and the student's alternatives, and work out how many buyers you need for ₹10,000, such as 10 at ₹999 (illustrative).
    5. Build the course pageShow the promise, a free demo lesson, the price with one button, what is included, who teaches, reviews and an FAQ, on your own domain.
    6. Turn on UPI checkoutAccept UPI and cards so students pay without messaging you and get access to the course automatically.
    7. Launch to your warm audienceRun a one-week launch on WhatsApp, YouTube and Instagram for past students, groups, subscribers and followers.
    8. Collect reviews and open batch 2Ask buyers for short reviews, use their doubts to improve the course, then add Meta ads and SEO to fill the next batch.

    Notice what is missing: a company registration, an agency-built app, a big ad budget and a 60-hour syllabus. They can come later. Selling is really three jobs: packaging (what outcome, for whom), infrastructure (where the course lives, how money and access work) and distribution (how a stranger finds it). Most educators spend all their energy on the first and barely try the third.

    Step 1: Pick a course people already ask you for

    Your first course should be the topic students already ask you about, written as one outcome, for one buyer, in one language. Demand you can see beats an idea you like.

    Look at the questions you answer again and again. An SSC maths teacher hears "Percentage aur Profit & Loss ka short trick course banao". A NEET biology tutor gets asked for a chapter-wise test series before the exam. A spoken English trainer hears "I freeze in interviews". A yoga teacher is asked for a 30-day back-pain routine. Each of those is a course waiting to be named.

    Write the offer in one sentence

    Include four things: the outcome, the buyer, the language and the time it takes.

    Too vagueReady to sell
    Maths courseSSC CGL Quant in Hindi: Percentage to Profit & Loss in 30 days
    NEET biologyNEET 2027 Biology: chapter-wise tests with video solutions, in Hinglish
    English speakingSpoken English for job interviews: 6 weeks of daily practice for freshers
    Yoga classes30-day yoga for desk workers with back pain, morning live sessions
     Topics describe a syllabus. Outcomes describe what the student gets.

    Students buy the removal of a worry (losing marks, freezing in an interview), not coverage of topics. Lead with the result; the syllabus is the proof.

    A narrow name is also easier to find: "Class 12 CBSE Physics numericals in Hinglish" matches a real search, while "Complete physics for all classes" competes with everything. If you teach in Hindi, Hinglish or a regional language, put the language in the course name too.

    Step 2: How to validate demand before you record

    Validate demand by finding the same request in at least two free places: your YouTube comments, a WhatsApp group poll, Google Trends and search suggestions. Then ask five real students whether they would pay. This takes a day or two and can save weeks of recording.

    1. YouTube comments

    Read the comments on your ten most-watched videos (or popular videos in your subject) and count requests for a full course, notes or a test series. "Sir, full course kab aayega?" repeated across videos is a strong signal.

    2. A WhatsApp group poll

    In a group of past or current students, post a poll with two or three options: "Which should the paid batch cover first: Percentage, Ratio or Time and Work?" Keep the options short. Everyone who votes is a likely early buyer.

    3. Google Trends

    Compare two topics or exams over the last 12 months in India. Google Trends does not show search counts; Google explains that each data point is divided by total searches for that place and time and then "scaled on a range of 0 to 100 based on a topic's proportion to all searches on all topics".1 Use it to compare interest, such as "ssc cgl" against "ssc chsl", or to see when interest peaks before an exam, not to estimate how many buyers exist.

    4. Search suggestions on Google and YouTube

    Type the start of your topic into Google and YouTube and note what they suggest: "spoken english for interview", "spoken english in hindi", "ssc maths tricks in hindi". Google says its autocomplete "predictions reflect real searches that have been done on Google".2 The suggestions show you the words students use, which become your course title and page headings.

    5. Ask five students the money question

    Send your one-sentence offer to five students who fit it and ask: "Would you pay ₹999 for this?" You are not looking for encouragement. You are looking for the objection: "Is there a test?", "Will doubts be cleared?", "Is it in Hindi?". That objection is what your course, demo lesson or price must answer.

    Step 3: Outline and record a minimum course

    A minimum course is the smallest complete course that fully delivers your one promise: a free demo lesson, 8 to 12 focused video lessons, PDF notes and a short test. It is small in scope, never unfinished in substance.

    Outline before you record

    Write the outline on one page, one idea per lesson. Exam courses follow the syllabus and end with a timed mock; skill courses follow practice: basics, drills, real use. Cut any lesson that does not move the student towards the promise.

    Record with what you have

    Record on the phone or laptop you already own. A clip-on microphone is the one purchase worth making, because students forgive plain video far more easily than poor sound. Use a whiteboard, a writing tablet or slides. If being on camera is what stops you, our guide on how to teach online without showing your face shows voice-and-screen formats that sell just as well.

    Add notes, a test and one live session

    A PDF of notes or a formula sheet, a short quiz after each part and a final mock make the course feel complete. One live doubt class a week, recorded into the course, answers the student's biggest worry: "What if I get stuck?"

    The word that matters is complete: ten lessons that fully get a student through SSC Percentage questions are a real product; forty that stop halfway are not.

    When educators set up an academy with us, the slowest step is rarely the technology. It is deciding what the first course is and recording the demo lesson. Give those two things a fixed deadline, such as two weeks.

    Step 4: How to price your first online course

    Price your first course against the result it gives and the student's other options, not against the hours you spent recording. Then work backwards from your first ₹10,000 to see how many buyers you need.

Do not give the whole course away to "build an audience": give away one excellent demo lesson instead.     Question educators ask

    Should my first course be free so people trust me?

    Give away a sample, not the product. One strong free lesson builds trust. A free course mostly attracts people who will wait for the next free thing. Even a small payment makes students more likely to finish and ask doubts.

    Step 5: Build a course page that sells

    A course page that sells answers six things in order: the promise, a free demo lesson, the price with one button, what is included, who teaches, and reviews with an FAQ. Put it on your own domain so you can share one link everywhere.

    1. One clear promise at the top: exam or skill, outcome, language and time.
    2. A free demo lesson so the student can judge your teaching before paying.
    3. The price and one button. No "DM for fees". One button that leads to checkout.
    4. What is included: number of lessons, notes, tests, live classes, validity and the app.
    5. Who teaches: your real experience in plain words. Never promise ranks or selection.
    6. Reviews and an FAQ that answer refund, validity, language and device questions.

    Most of your students will open the page on a phone, often from a WhatsApp link. Keep the top of the page short, make the button easy to tap, and test it on a budget Android phone. Our guide on how to create a landing page for an online course covers each part with examples.

    A web address such as youracademy.in/ssc-maths-hindi looks like a real academy and stays yours if you change tools later. Every AllCoaching plan includes connecting your own domain, so the course page, checkout and your app all carry your name.

    Step 6: Take payments by UPI, with access on payment

    Indian students expect to pay by UPI, so your checkout should accept UPI and cards, and open the course automatically once the payment succeeds. A QR code in a WhatsApp chat and a Google Drive link is not a checkout.

    UPI is how India pays. NPCI data reported by Outlook Money shows UPI processed 24.07 billion transactions worth ₹29.37 lakh crore in September 2026 alone (checked October 2026).3 Asking students to do anything harder at checkout loses sales.

    Why WhatsApp QR and Drive links break

    The manual setup breaks by the third buyer: you check screenshots, add people by hand and send Drive links that get forwarded. There is no record of who bought what, no way to remove access, and nothing that tells a stranger this is a real academy.

    A proper checkout fixes all of that: the student pays by UPI or card, access opens on its own, and you get a record of every sale. Our guide on how to accept UPI payments for online courses explains gateways, KYC and charges.

    On AllCoaching, UPI and card checkout is built into every plan, with automatic payouts through Razorpay. The student pays on your course page or in your app and starts learning straight away.

    One note on records: course income is taxable income, and GST rules depend on your turnover and situation. Keep clean records from the first sale and read GST and income tax for online tutors in India, or ask your accountant.

    Step 7: Launch to your warm audience on WhatsApp, YouTube and Instagram

    Your first buyers will almost always come from people who already know you: past students, WhatsApp groups, YouTube subscribers and Instagram followers. Plan a one-week launch for them before you spend anything on ads.

    Who is in your warm audience

    List everyone who has seen you teach: former students, parents, your groups, subscribers, followers and colleagues. Even a few hundred people who trust you can be enough for 5 to 10 first buyers (illustrative). They are also mostly on their phones: TRAI reported 1,114.17 million internet subscribers in India at the end of June 2026, of whom 1,066.43 million were wireless (checked October 2026).4

    WhatsApp: conversation and reminders

    Announce the offer and course link in your groups, send a reminder before the start date and reply to every question personally. For larger lists, see how educators use WhatsApp Channels for coaching.

    YouTube: proof and reach

    Post the demo lesson with the course link in the description and pinned comment, and hold a short YouTube Live Q&A. If YouTube is your main channel, our guide to moving YouTube viewers into your own academy goes further.

    Instagram: trust in short form

    Share short clips, a peek at the notes and doubts you answered, with the course link in your bio and stories.

    YouTube and Instagram are where people meet you, WhatsApp is where you talk, and your course page is where they pay. Turn followers on rented platforms into a student list in your own academy that you can contact when batch 2 opens.

    Question educators ask

    I have no followers. Who will buy my first course?

    Start with people who have seen you teach: past students, parents, colleagues and local groups. Share one strong free lesson and the course link with them personally. If even that list is very small, spend a few weeks posting free lessons on YouTube or Instagram first, then launch.

    Step 8: After the first sale: reviews, word of mouth and batch 2

    After your first sales, ask every happy student for a short review, help them tell friends, use their doubts to improve the course, and open the next batch on the same page. The first students are how you find the next ones.

    • Ask for reviews early. Two lines and a result ("cleared my doubts on Profit & Loss in a week") while it is fresh. Add them to the course page.
    • Make word of mouth easy. Ask happy students to share the course link with one friend preparing for the same exam. Personal recommendations travel fast in exam groups.
    • Use their doubts. The questions your first buyers ask are your next lessons, your FAQ and your next course.
    • Open batch 2. Same page, new start date, better reviews. A dated batch gives students a reason to decide now.

    When to add paid ads and SEO

    Add paid ads after your course has sold to a warm audience, and start SEO early because it takes months to build. Ads can scale what already works; they rarely fix an offer nobody wanted.

    First paid ads on Facebook and Instagram

    Once 5 to 10 people have bought, you know your offer and the buyers' questions, and a small Meta ads test makes sense: a reel that teaches something real, sent to the course page, with Meta Pixel tracking purchases. Judge it by cost per enrolment (ad spend divided by paid enrolments), never by likes.     Mistakes that stop the first sale

    The first sale is usually blocked by the setup around the course, not by the teaching. The five that come up most: recording 60 lessons before anyone has paid, a course "for everyone", fees through a WhatsApp QR with Drive links, ads before a single organic sale, and waiting for a big following before launching.

    Glossary

    Minimum course
    The smallest complete course that fully delivers one promise, such as a demo lesson, 8 to 12 focused videos, PDF notes and a short test.
    Demand validation
    Checking that students want a course before recording it, using signals such as repeated comments, poll answers, search suggestions and students saying they would pay.
    Warm audience
    People who already know your teaching: past students, parents, WhatsApp groups, YouTube subscribers and Instagram followers.
    Course page
    The web page for one course that states the promise, shows a demo lesson, lists what is included and leads to checkout.
    UPI checkout
    A payment step where a student pays by UPI (or card) on your course page and gets access automatically when the payment succeeds.
    Price ladder
    A set of products at rising prices, such as a notes pack, a test series, a recorded course and a course with live doubt classes.
    Platform fee
    The percentage of each sale an academy platform keeps. On AllCoaching it is 0% on every monthly plan, or 10%, 7.5% or 5% on the yearly plans.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I sell my course online in India?

    Pick a course students already ask for, check demand in comments, polls and search suggestions, record a small complete course, put it on a course page with UPI checkout, and launch it to people who know you on WhatsApp, YouTube and Instagram.

    How many students do I need to earn my first ₹10,000?

    It depends on your price. As an illustration, 10 students at ₹999 or 5 at ₹1,999 is about ₹10,000 in sales, before platform fees, payment charges and taxes.

    Do I need a website or app to sell an online course?

    Not for a first test sale, but once sales grow you need a course page with checkout and automatic access. An academy platform gives you both, plus an app, without a developer.

    How long does it take to make the first online course sale?

    For a focused educator with a warm audience, roughly four to six weeks is common as an illustration: a few days to pick and validate the course, one to two weeks to record, and a launch week.

    What price should my first online course be in India?

    Price against the result and the student's alternatives, not your recording hours. Many educators start with a notes pack or test series in the hundreds of rupees and a recorded course from about ₹499 to ₹2,999 (illustrative ranges).

    Can I sell courses online in Hindi?

    Yes. Name the language in the course title, such as SSC CGL Maths in Hindi, and teach in the language your students search in. Hindi and Hinglish courses can stand out from English-first options.

    Should I sell on WhatsApp or on a proper platform?

    Use WhatsApp to talk to students and share the link, but take payments on a course page with UPI checkout. Manual QR payments and Drive links break as sales grow and give no control over access.

    Should I run Facebook or Instagram ads for my first course?

    Usually not before your first organic sales. Sell to your warm audience first, learn the objections, then test a small Meta ads budget with Meta Pixel tracking and judge it by cost per enrolment.
