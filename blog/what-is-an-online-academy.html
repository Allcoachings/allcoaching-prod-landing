    What is an online academy?

    An online academy is an educator's or institute's own school on the internet: a branded website and app where students discover courses, pay, and learn through recorded lessons, PDF notes, tests and live classes. Unlike a single course, it holds many courses, batches and students under one name, and keeps them coming back.

    Put simply, the academy is the business and the courses are what it sells. A student does not join "Chapter 4 video". They join your academy, for a NEET 2027 batch, and then buy your test series and a crash course before the exam.

    Three things separate an online academy from other ways of teaching online:

    • One brand. Students see your name, logo and web address, not a platform's.
    • Many products. Courses, test series, notes, live batches and memberships live side by side.
    • A relationship that lasts. Students log in again and again, and you know who they are.

    Online academy meaning in Hindi and everyday use

    In everyday Indian usage, "online academy" overlaps with "online coaching", "online institute" and "online classes". In Hindi you will hear it called an online coaching centre or online shiksha sansthan. The idea is the same: a teacher or institute that runs its classes, notes, tests and fees online instead of (or as well as) in a classroom.

    The word "academy" matters, though. It signals something more permanent than a one-off course: a place with a name, a syllabus, a calendar and a community of students.

    Online academy vs online course, YouTube channel and marketplace

    An online course is one product; an online academy is the whole school that sells many products under your brand. A YouTube channel builds a free audience, and a marketplace listing puts your course in someone else's shop. An academy is where those audiences end up paying you directly.

    Online academy vs online course

    A single online course is a set of lessons on one topic, such as "SSC CGL Quant in Hindi". It can be sold as a link, a Google Drive folder or a page on a course platform. That works for a first experiment.

    An academy starts to matter when you have more than one thing to sell, or more than one batch a year. It keeps every course, every student and every payment in one place, so a student who finished your Quant course can buy your mock-test series in two taps.

    Online academy vs YouTube channel

    YouTube is excellent for being found. It is not built for paid learning: there is no syllabus order, no test series, no fee collection for a batch and no way to see which student watched what. Most successful educators in India use YouTube as the front door and an academy as the classroom. Our guide on how to sell courses online in India shows how to move viewers from free videos to a paid course.

    Online academy vs marketplace listing (Udemy or Unacademy style)

    On a large marketplace, your course is one listing among thousands. The platform owns the student relationship, sets much of the pricing and branding, and the student remembers the platform more than you. In return, you may get discovery. In your own academy, the student relationship, the price and the brand are yours. If you are weighing the two, read own app or marketplace: three paths compared.

    Question educators ask

    Can I run an online academy and still sell on YouTube or a marketplace?

    Yes, and many educators do. Free YouTube lessons and social posts bring students in; the academy is where they pay, learn and stay. The key is that the paid relationship sits on a website and app with your name on it.

    Online academy vs LMS: what is the difference?

    An LMS (learning management system) is the software that delivers and tracks learning; an online academy is the complete business built around it. Every online academy needs an LMS inside it, but an LMS on its own is not an academy.

    Wikipedia describes a learning management system as "a software application for the administration, documentation, tracking, reporting, automation, and delivery of educational courses, training programs, materials, or learning and development programs".1 That covers lessons, tests and progress. It does not, by itself, cover the things that make students find you, trust you and pay you.

    The parts of an online academy

    A complete online academy has seven parts: a website on your own domain, courses, live classes, payments, a student app, student data and marketing tools. You can start without some of them, but students notice when one is missing.

    1. A website on your own domain

    Your academy's address, such as youracademy.in, is the one link you share everywhere: YouTube descriptions, Instagram bio, WhatsApp groups and posters. A domain in your name keeps that address yours even if you change tools later. Our guide to an LMS with a custom domain explains how connecting one works.

    2. Courses: video, PDF notes and tests

    Most Indian academies sell a mix: recorded video lessons in syllabus order, downloadable PDF notes and formula sheets, and test series with timed mocks and solutions. Exam-prep students in particular expect previous-year questions and full-length mocks.

    3. Live classes

    Live batches, doubt-clearing sessions and revision marathons keep students accountable. They can run on YouTube Live, Zoom or a live room inside the academy, with recordings added to the course afterwards. See how to conduct live classes on mobile apps for the practical setup.

    4. Payments

    Students pay by UPI or card at checkout, and access opens automatically. No screenshots of payment on WhatsApp, no manual adding to groups. If you are choosing a gateway, compare charges in our guide to the best UPI payment gateway for online courses.

    5. A student app

    Indian students learn mostly on phones. TRAI reported 1,114.17 million internet subscribers in India at the end of June 2026, of whom 1,066.43 million were wireless subscribers (checked October 2026).3 An app with your name on the student's home screen is a daily reminder of your academy.

    6. Student data

    Who enrolled, who paid, who finished Chapter 6 and who has not logged in for two weeks. This record is what lets you follow up, run a re-enrolment offer, or move platforms without losing anyone. Make sure you can export it.

    7. Marketing tools

    Pages that can rank on Google, plus tracking such as Meta Pixel and Google Tag Manager so your ads and posts can be measured. Our online academy SEO guide for beginners covers the search side in detail.

    Types of online academy (with examples from India)

    Most online academies in India fall into six types: exam-prep, skill, school tuition, cohort or bootcamp, membership, and an offline institute going online. Many educators combine two, such as an exam-prep academy with a membership for current affairs.

    1. Exam-prep academy

    Built around a competitive exam and its calendar: NEET, JEE, SSC CGL, banking PO, UPSC, state PSC, CUET or CLAT. The products are full courses, subject-wise batches, test series and crash courses timed to the exam. Hindi and regional-medium academies are a large share of this type. Example: a physics teacher in Lucknow running a NEET 2027 batch in Hinglish, with weekly tests. Our guide for NEET educators goes deeper.

    2. Skill academy

    Teaches a skill rather than a syllabus: spoken English, yoga, Carnatic or Hindustani music, guitar, coding, digital marketing, design, vastu or the Vedas. Students are often working adults, and they buy on outcomes ("speak confidently in interviews"). Example: a spoken-English trainer selling a 60-day course and weekly live speaking practice. See our guide for spoken English teachers.

    3. School-tuition academy

    Follows CBSE, ICSE or state-board syllabi for Classes 6 to 12. Parents are often the buyers, so clear schedules, test results and progress reports matter. Example: a maths tutor teaching Class 10 board batches online after school hours.

    4. Cohort or bootcamp academy

    Runs fixed-date batches, usually live-first, for a set number of weeks. Everyone starts and finishes together. It suits intensive programmes such as a 6-week interview-preparation bootcamp or a 30-day UPSC answer-writing challenge.

    5. Membership academy

    Students pay for ongoing access rather than a single course: monthly current affairs for UPSC, a yoga practice library, or a music riyaz community with new lessons each week. Income is steadier, but you must keep adding content.

    6. Institute going online

    An established offline coaching centre that adds online batches, recorded backups of classroom lectures and online test series. It keeps its local reputation and reaches students in other cities. Our guide to moving offline coaching online covers the switch.

    How does an online academy work?

    An online academy works like an online shop for teaching: you set it up once, add courses, share one link, and the platform handles payments, student access, the app and hosting each time someone enrols. Your daily work stays teaching.

    What you do

    1. Sign up on an academy platform and name your academy.
    2. Choose a plan that fits your stage and expected sales.
    3. Upload your course: videos, PDFs and tests, in syllabus order, with a free demo lesson.
    4. Connect your domain and add your logo and colours.
    5. Start selling: share the course link with your students and followers.

    What runs on its own

    When a student pays, the checkout collects the fee by UPI or card, access to the course opens, the student can log in on the website or your app, and the site stays online without you managing servers. This is the difference between an academy and a stitched-together setup of separate tools.

    Here is an illustrative day for a JEE maths educator. In the morning, two students buy the crash course from a link in last night's YouTube video; both get access without a message to you. At 6 pm you run a live doubt session that is recorded into the course. At night you check which students skipped this week's mock and send them a reminder. None of these steps needs a spreadsheet.

    What does an online academy cost in India?

    An online academy's cost is the sum of five things: the platform plan, any fee the platform takes on sales, your domain, payment-gateway charges and GST. Content production (a mic, lighting and your time) comes on top. Add all of them before comparing platforms.

    The generic cost lines

    • Platform plan. A yearly or monthly price for the software. Check whether GST is included or extra.
    • Fee on sales. Some platforms keep a percentage of each sale; others charge only the plan. A percentage costs little when sales are small and more as you grow.
    • Domain. Bought from a domain registrar and renewed every year. Register it in your own name so it stays yours.
    • Payment gateway. Gateways charge per transaction. Razorpay's standard plan lists a 2% platform fee per successful domestic transaction, with 18% GST on that fee (checked October 2026).4 Ask your academy platform how gateway charges are handled.
    • GST. Platform bills usually carry GST, and if you are GST-registered your own course fees may too. Our guide to GST for coaching institutes explains the rules.

    Is an online academy right for you?

    An online academy is right for you if you already teach something people pay for and want students to remember your name, not a platform's. It is too early if you have not yet taught the topic or have no time to teach each week.

    Good fit

    You teach an exam or skill with steady demand. Students already message you questions on WhatsApp or comment on your YouTube videos. You can teach live or record a few lessons a week. You plan to run more than one batch or course.

    Wait a little

    You have not taught the topic to real students yet. You have no syllabus or sample lessons. You want income without teaching. In these cases, teach a small group first, even for free, then build the academy around what worked.

    Glossary

    Online academy
    An educator's or institute's own branded school on the internet, where students buy courses, learn, take tests and attend live classes.
    Online course
    A single set of lessons on one topic, sold or shared online. An academy can sell many courses.
    LMS (learning management system)
    Software that delivers lessons and tests and tracks student progress. It is the engine inside an online academy.
    Custom domain
    Your own web address, such as youracademy.in, connected to your academy website instead of a platform's address.
    White-label app
    A student app that carries your academy's name and logo, built on a platform's technology.
    Test series
    A set of timed mock tests with answers and solutions, common in exam-prep academies.
    Cohort
    A group of students who start and finish a batch on the same dates, usually with live classes.
    Platform fee
    A percentage of each sale that an academy platform keeps, in addition to or instead of a plan price.
    Payment gateway
    The service that collects UPI, card and net-banking payments online and settles them to your bank account, for a per-transaction charge.
