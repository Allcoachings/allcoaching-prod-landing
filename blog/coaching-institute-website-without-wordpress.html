    Why build a coaching website without WordPress?

    Because a coaching website is not just pages: it has to sell courses, take payments, log students in and play videos, and on WordPress each of those is a separate plugin or service you must set up and keep updated.     What pages does a coaching institute website need?

    A coaching institute website needs seven pages: a home page, course pages with prices, an about-the-teacher page, a results page only if your results are real, free content, a contact page with WhatsApp, and policy pages. Everything else is optional at the start.

    1. Home

    Say who you teach, what, and in which language in the first line: "NEET Biology in Hindi for Class 11 and 12". Add one button to your courses and your WhatsApp number. A student should know within five seconds whether this site is for them.

    2. Courses with prices

    One page per course: who it is for, what is inside (lessons, PDFs, tests, live classes), validity, language, price and a buy button. Hiding the price behind "call for fees" costs you the students who browse at 11 pm and never call. Our guide to pricing online courses in India helps you set it.

    3. About the teacher

    A clear photo, your years of teaching, the exams or skills you cover, and your method in plain words. Parents read this page more carefully than any other.

    4. Results, only if real

    Show selections, scores or student reviews only when they are real, recent and shared with the student's permission. Borrowed topper photos and invented numbers damage trust the moment a parent checks. If you are new, skip this page and let free content do the work.

    5. Free content

    A free demo lesson, a chapter PDF or a short test lets a student judge your teaching before paying. It also gives Google something useful to index.

    6. Contact and WhatsApp

    A WhatsApp button that opens a chat in one tap, a phone number, your centre's address and a map if you teach offline. Many Indian students will message before they buy.

    7. Policies

    Privacy policy, terms of use and a refund policy, linked in the footer. More on these in the legal pages section below.

    Question educators ask

    Should I put student testimonials on my coaching website?

    Yes, if they are real. Ask the student in writing, use their actual words, and add their exam and year. A short WhatsApp message from a real student, shared with permission, builds more trust than a polished quote nobody can verify. Never write testimonials yourself or reuse photos of toppers from other institutes.

    How does a coaching website get found on Google?

    A coaching website gets found on Google through clear course pages that match what students type, a plain title and description on every page, structured data, and helpful free content that links to your courses. You do not need plugins for any of this.

    Start with the course page. Name it the way students search: "SSC CGL Maths Course in Hindi", not "Batch 7 Premium". Google's documentation explains that it "uses structured data that it finds on the web to understand the content of the page".4 On WordPress, that usually means another SEO plugin. On AllCoaching, every course page gets SEO meta and schema when you save the course, and the academy-wide SEO title, description and sharing image sit in the studio's preferences.

    • One page per course, with the exam, subject and language in the title.
    • Syllabus as text, not an image, so Google and students can read it.
    • A short FAQ on each course page answering validity, language and doubt support.
    • Blog posts that answer exam questions (syllabus, cut-offs, study plans) and link to the course.
    • A Google Business Profile if you also teach from a physical centre.

    Why a coaching website must be mobile-first

    Your students, especially in tier-2 and tier-3 towns, will open your website on a phone, often an inexpensive Android phone on mobile data, and Google also judges your site by its mobile version. Design and check for the phone first, then the laptop.

    Google Search Central puts it plainly: "Google uses the mobile version of a site's content, crawled with the smartphone agent, for indexing and ranking."5 If your course details or prices are hidden on the phone view, Google may not see them either.

    Open your site on the oldest phone in your family, on mobile data. Can you read the text without zooming? Is the price easy to find? Does WhatsApp open in one tap? Can you pay by UPI without getting lost? If a Hindi-medium student is your audience, are the key lines in Hindi?

    The next step on a phone is an app. Students who study daily come back more easily from an icon than a bookmark. On WordPress, an app is a separate project.

    Which legal pages does a coaching website need?

    Before you sell, add a privacy policy, terms of use, a refund and cancellation policy, and a contact page with your real name, address, phone and email. Payment gateways often ask for these pages when they review your website, and parents look for the refund policy before paying a large fee.

    • Privacy policy: what student data you collect (name, phone, email, payments), why, and how a student can ask you to correct or delete it. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 applies to how you handle that data.
    • Terms of use: course validity, account sharing, recording rules and what happens if a batch is rescheduled.
    • Refund and cancellation policy: whether refunds are possible, within how many days, and how to ask. Clear rules prevent most disputes.
    • Contact: a real address and phone number. A website with only a form looks less trustworthy than one with a person behind it.

    Create these as normal pages with the page builder and link them in the footer. Have a lawyer or CA review them once; a template copied from another institute may promise things you do not do.

    When does WordPress still make sense for a coaching institute?

    WordPress still makes sense if you already have a developer who maintains it, if your site is mainly a large blog or news publication, or if you need a specific plugin no academy platform offers. It is a flexible tool; the question is whether you want to run it yourself.

    WordPress fits when

    • A developer or agency already maintains your site and you are happy with it.
    • Your site is mostly articles, news or exam updates, with few paid courses.
    • You need a niche plugin or custom feature for your institute.
    • You enjoy managing hosting, themes and updates yourself.

    A hosted academy fits when

    • You want to sell courses, tests and live classes, not only publish pages.
    • Nobody on your team wants to handle updates and security.
    • You want your own Android app without a separate project.
    • You want one login for website, payments, students and content.

    A middle path also works: keep an existing WordPress site for news and articles, and link its "Courses" button to your academy for courses, payments and student login. When you are ready to move everything, copy your page text across, bulk-import your students from Excel or Google Sheet, recreate important course URLs where it makes sense, and point your domain at the academy.

    Glossary

    Content management system (CMS)
    Software for creating and editing website pages without writing code, such as WordPress.
    Plugin
    An add-on installed into WordPress to add a feature such as courses, payments or SEO, which must be updated along with WordPress.
    Hosted academy platform
    A service that runs your academy website, courses, student login, payments and hosting for you, so there is nothing to install or update.
    Landing page
    A single page built for one campaign, such as an admission drive or a free webinar, with one clear action.
    Structured data (schema)
    Code on a page that describes its content, such as a course name and price, in a standard format search engines read.
    Mobile-first indexing
    Google's practice of using the mobile version of a page for indexing and ranking.
    Refund policy
    A page that states whether, when and how students can get their fees back.
    Custom domain
    Your own web address, such as youracademy.in, connected to your academy website instead of a platform's address.
