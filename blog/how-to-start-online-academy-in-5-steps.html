    Older guides to starting an online academy list fifteen or more steps: buy hosting, install WordPress, pick an LMS plugin, add a payment plugin, set up a video host, wire up email, build an app. Each of those is still a real job. The difference in 2026 is that an academy platform does most of them for you, so the steps left are the ones only you can do: deciding what to teach, recording it and telling students about it.

    When educators set up an academy with us, the slowest step is almost never the software. It is Step 1: deciding what the first course is and recording the demo lesson. So that is where we start.

    What do you need before you start an online academy?

    You need one topic you teach well, a syllabus for one course, one demo lesson, a phone with a clip-on mic, PAN and a bank account for payments, a domain name, a simple logo and a few hours a week. You do not need a developer, a studio or a big budget.

    Two items catch people out. The first is payment KYC: any payment gateway will ask for identity and bank details before it settles money to you, so keep your PAN, bank account and address proof ready. Our guide to accepting UPI payments for online courses lists the documents. The second is the domain: buy it yourself, in your own name, rather than letting a vendor or agency hold it.

    If today your academy runs on a mix of WhatsApp, Drive, Zoom and Excel, you already have the most important things: students and content. Starting an online academy is mostly moving them into one place.

    Step 1: Plan your academy and your first course

    Before you sign up anywhere, write down what you teach, to whom, in which language, and what your first course will contain. This one page decides your course title, your price, your website copy and the students who find you.

    Answer four questions on one page

    • What do you teach? The exam, subject or skill, and the level. "Competitive exams" is too broad; "SSC CGL Quant" is a course.
    • For whom? Class 12 droppers, working adults, Class 10 board students, first-time job seekers.
    • In which language? English, Hindi, Hinglish or a regional language. Many students search in the language they want to be taught in.
    • Why you? Your method, your results with past batches, or a format others do not offer.

    Specific beats general. "I teach competitive exam preparation" gives a student nothing to search for. "Hindi-medium NEET biology for Class 12 repeaters, with chapter-wise PDF notes and weekly tests" tells them exactly who it is for, and gives Google words to match. The same works for skills: "Spoken English for interview preparation, for freshers in tier-2 cities" is a course people can picture buying.

    Pick one first course, not ten

    Choose the course you have already taught to real students, and build it as modules: video lessons in syllabus order, a PDF for each chapter, and a test at the end of each module. Exam students expect timed mock tests and solutions; skill students expect practice tasks and feedback.

    Record a demo lesson

    Your demo lesson is the one free lesson every student sees before paying. Make it 10 to 20 minutes, teach one real concept fully, and end with what the full course covers. Students cannot judge your teaching from a price; they judge it from this lesson. For recording on a phone with good audio, see our guide to running classes from a mobile phone.

    Set a price range

    Look at what students in your exam or skill already pay, then decide where your course sits. A recorded course, a test series and a live batch usually sit at different prices. Our guide on how to price online courses in India covers the methods, including launch pricing for your first batch.

    Question educators ask

    I have only two hours a week. Can I still start an online academy?

    Yes, with a slower timeline. An illustrative plan: week one, write the one-page plan and sign up; week two, record the demo lesson and two more lessons; week three, upload the first module with its PDF and test, and connect your domain; week four, share the link and run one live demo class. Launch with one finished module and add the next each week, rather than waiting until the whole course is recorded.

    Step 3: Upload your first course

    Create the course, add its videos, PDF notes and tests in syllabus order, mark one lesson as the free demo, set the price and publish. Keep it in draft until a friend or past student has gone through it once.

    Videos: record once, host on YouTube

    Record lessons of 8 to 20 minutes, one concept each. Long recordings of a full classroom lecture are harder to watch on a phone and harder to find later. On AllCoaching, videos are added through YouTube, with no limit on the number. You can keep them unlisted: YouTube's help centre says unlisted videos "can be seen and shared by anyone with the link" and do not appear in search results or on your channel's Videos tab.1 Your free YouTube videos stay public and bring new students in.

    PDF notes and tests

    Add one PDF per chapter: notes, formula sheets, practice questions or previous-year papers. Then add tests. For exam students, a chapter test after each module and full-length timed mocks at the end are what they expect.

    Live classes

    Most Indian academies mix recorded lessons with live sessions: a weekly doubt class, a revision marathon before the exam, or a full live batch. Every AllCoaching plan includes unlimited live classes through YouTube Live, Zoom or the built-in live room, so you can schedule them alongside the recorded course.

    Name, price and description

    Use the course title students would search for ("SSC CGL Quant in Hindi: Complete Course"), say who it is for in the first line of the description, list what is inside (number of lessons, PDFs and tests), and set the price you decided in Step 1. Then buy your own course once as a test before you share it.

    Step 4: Connect your domain and make it your brand

    Buy a domain such as youracademy.in in your own name, add the DNS records your platform shows you at your registrar, then add your logo and colours. From then on, every link you share carries your name, not a platform's.

    Choose and buy the domain

    Pick something short that students can type after hearing it once: your name, your academy's name, or your subject plus a word. A .in or .com address both work. Buy it from any registrar, in your own name and with your own email, so it stays yours if you ever change platforms.

    Add the DNS records

    Your academy platform shows the exact records to add, usually a TXT record that proves you own the domain and a CNAME record that points it at the academy. Log in to your registrar, open DNS settings, and copy the values exactly. The change usually takes effect within minutes to a few hours, and occasionally up to a day or two. Our guide to an LMS with a custom domain covers the details and common errors.

    Brand the academy

    Add your logo, two brand colours and a theme. A clear photo of you and a short paragraph on who you teach do more for trust than any design. On AllCoaching, every plan includes customisable themes and your own white-label Android app with your name and logo; the iOS app is included on Pro and Scale.

    Google's own SEO starter guide notes that parts of a URL can appear in search results as breadcrumbs, and suggests including "words in the URL that may be useful for users".2 So name course pages plainly, such as youracademy.in/ssc-cgl-quant-hindi.

    Step 5: Start selling

    Share one link to your course everywhere your students already are, and let the checkout do the rest: the student pays by UPI or card, access opens, and the money reaches your bank. Your job in launch week is to bring people to the link and answer their questions.

    How a sale works

    Indian students pay by UPI first. NPCI data reported in October 2026 showed 24.07 billion UPI transactions worth ₹29.37 lakh crore in September 2026 alone.3 If your checkout takes UPI, almost every student can pay in a few taps. On AllCoaching, UPI and card checkout and automatic Razorpay payouts are built in.

    Gateways charge per transaction. Razorpay's standard plan, for example, lists a 2% platform fee per successful domestic transaction, with GST on that fee (checked October 2026).4 Count this alongside your academy plan when you work out what each sale earns you. The full setup is in our guide to accepting UPI payments for online courses.

    Launch week

    • Test first. Buy your own course, open it on an inexpensive Android phone, and fix anything confusing.
    • Add tracking. Every AllCoaching plan includes Meta Pixel and Google Tag Manager, so you can see which posts and ads bring sales.
    • Share the link. YouTube descriptions and pinned comments, your Instagram bio, your WhatsApp groups and channel, and your Telegram group if you have one.
    • Run a free live demo class. Teach one topic properly, then explain the course and share the link at the end.
    • Reply to everything. The first students ask about validity, language, doubt support and refunds. Their questions tell you what your course page is missing.

    Question educators ask

    My students only use WhatsApp and do not want to install another app. What do I do?

    Keep WhatsApp for conversation and send students to the website link for payment and lessons. The website works in the phone's browser, and the app can come later once they see the value. Our guide on students who only want WhatsApp has practical scripts.

    What to do in the first 30 days after launch

    In the first month, collect feedback from your first students, improve the course page, publish free content that leads to the course, and start tracking where sales come from. Growth after launch comes from steady, repeated work, not one big announcement.

    Improve from real questions

    Write down every question your first students ask before and after buying. Add the answers to your course description and FAQ. If several students drop off at the same lesson, re-record it.

    Make your academy findable on Google

    Write a few helpful pages around your exam or skill, link them to your course, and keep the titles plain. Expect it to take time: Google's SEO starter guide says some changes "might take effect in a few hours, others could take several months".2 Our online academy SEO guide for beginners shows where to start.

    Try a small ad test

    Once the Pixel has recorded a few visits and sales, a small Meta ad budget pointed at your demo lesson or live demo class can show you which message works. Start small and measure cost per sale, not likes. See Meta ads for coaching institutes for a step-by-step setup.

    Plan the second product

    When the first course sells, add the obvious next purchase: a test series for the same exam, a crash course before the exam date, or the next level of the skill. For more ways to move YouTube viewers to paid courses, read how to sell courses online in India.

    Common mistakes when starting an online academy

    The mistakes that slow a launch are teaching everyone, building ten courses before selling one, skipping the free demo, waiting to be perfect, letting someone else own your domain, and collecting fees on WhatsApp. Each has a simple fix.

    • Teaching everyone. A general "all competitive exams" academy is hard to search for and hard to trust. Start with one exam or skill and one language.
    • Ten courses on day one. One finished course you can sell this month is worth more than ten outlines. Add the next course when the first one sells.
    • No free demo lesson. Students cannot judge your teaching from a price tag. Give them one full lesson.
    • Waiting to be perfect. Launch with one finished module and improve with real feedback. A launched course teaches you more than a polished draft.
    • Domain in a vendor's name. If an agency or platform owns your web address, you cannot easily leave. Register it yourself.
    • Selling only on WhatsApp. Payment screenshots and manual group adds do not scale and leave no clean record. Send every student to checkout.

    Glossary

    Online academy
    An educator's or institute's own branded school on the internet, where students buy courses, learn, take tests and attend live classes.
    Demo lesson
    One full lesson given free so students can judge your teaching before they pay for the course.
    Custom domain
    Your own web address, such as youracademy.in, connected to your academy website instead of a platform's address.
    DNS record
    A setting at your domain registrar that tells the internet where your domain points. Connecting an academy usually needs a TXT record and a CNAME record.
    Unlisted video
    A YouTube video that anyone with the link can watch but that does not appear in search results or on your channel page.
    White-label app
    A student app that carries your academy's name and logo, built on a platform's technology.
    Platform fee
    A percentage of each sale that an academy platform keeps, in addition to or instead of a plan price.
    Payment gateway
    The service that collects UPI, card and net-banking payments online and settles them to your bank account, for a per-transaction charge.
    Meta Pixel
    A tracking code from Meta added to your website so you can measure which Facebook and Instagram ads lead to visits and sales.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I start an online academy?

    Start an online academy in five steps: plan one course for one clear group of students, sign up on an academy platform and choose a plan, upload the course with videos, PDF notes and tests, connect your own domain, and start selling through a checkout that takes UPI and cards.

    How do I start an online academy in India with no technical skills?

    Use an academy platform that handles the website, app, payments and hosting for you. You add your content, connect a domain by copying two DNS records at your registrar, and share your course link. No coding or developer is needed.

    How long does it take to start an online academy?

    Signing up takes minutes. Most of the time goes into planning and recording your first course. One educator with one course can usually launch within one to two weeks; with only a few hours a week, plan for about a month.

    How much does it cost to start an online academy in India?

    Add the platform plan, any platform fee on sales, payment gateway charges, a yearly domain and GST. On AllCoaching, after a 14-day free trial with no card, plans cost ₹6,999, ₹12,999 or ₹24,999 a year with a 10%, 7.5% or 5% fee on sales, or ₹99,999 a year with 0%, all excluding GST.

    What should my first online course include?

    Video lessons in syllabus order, one PDF of notes per chapter, a test after each module, and one free demo lesson. Exam courses should add full-length timed mock tests; skill courses should add practice tasks.

    Do I need my own domain for an online academy?

    It is strongly recommended. A domain such as youracademy.in is the one link you share everywhere, and if it is registered in your own name it stays yours even if you change platforms later.

    Can I use YouTube videos in my paid online course?

    Yes. Many educators upload lessons to YouTube as unlisted videos, which only people with the link can see, and add them to the paid course. Public YouTube videos then work as free lessons that bring new students to the academy.

    How do students pay in an online academy?

    Through a checkout on your academy website or app. The student pays by UPI or card, the payment gateway confirms it, course access opens automatically, and the money is settled to your bank account.

    Do I need a mobile app for my online academy?

    A website on your own domain is enough to start, and it works in any phone browser. An app with your name helps students come back daily. On AllCoaching every plan includes your own Android app, and the iOS app is included on Pro and Scale.
