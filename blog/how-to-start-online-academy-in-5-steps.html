Step 1 — Define Your Brand & Student Persona

1

Foundational Step · 60–90 minutes

Get Specific About What You Teach and Who You Teach It To

Before you sign up on any platform — before you upload a single PDF — answer four questions on a single page. What do you teach (subject, exam, level, topics)? For whom (class level, exam target, learning stage)? In which language (English, Hindi, Hinglish, regional)? What is your differentiated value (pedagogy, format strength, track record)? This page is your academy's positioning. Everything that follows is built on top of it.

This step looks soft, but it is operationally the most consequential decision in the entire launch. Specificity is no longer a marketing nicety in 2026 — it is a discovery requirement. AI recommendation systems and AI search engines surface educators who match precise student intent, not educators with generic descriptions. An academy that positions as "I teach competitive exam preparation" will not be surfaced. An academy that positions as "I teach Hindi-medium NEET biology with chapter-wise PDF notes, weekly chapter tests, and a full-length mock series for Class 12 repeaters" will be.

Do this in 90 minutes. Open a blank document. Write four short paragraphs answering the four questions above. Do not aim for perfect — aim for specific. You will refine this over months as you see which segments respond. But you must enter Step 2 with a written persona, not a vague intent. Educators who skip this step launch generic academies that AI systems cannot meaningfully recommend.

If you teach NEET, decide which subject and which class. If you teach UPSC, decide which optional subject and which language medium. If you teach K-12, decide which class and which board. The discipline of narrowness is not a constraint — it is what makes the rest of the academy discoverable, payable, and scalable. Generic positioning is the single most expensive mistake new educators make in 2026.

Step 3 — Build & Publish Your Multi-Format Catalog

3

The Build Step · 1–3 days

Upload Across Multiple Formats Under a Unified Profile

Modern Indian students do not buy single-format content. They buy PDF notes, handwritten notes, chapter-wise test series, full-length mock tests, MCQ banks, recorded video courses, live class schedules, and bundled course packages — all from the same educator. Upload across at least three formats on day one. Tag every piece with exam category, subject, language, level, and price.

The strategic insight most online academy advice misses is that multi-format catalogs are not just a revenue diversification — they are a discovery and retention multiplier. A student who finds your test series may also enroll in your recorded course; a student who likes your PDFs is far more likely to commit to your live classes. Each format becomes a discovery surface for the others. The educator who launches with one format leaves most of their lifetime value on the table from day one.

Recommended Day-One Catalog

Three Formats Minimum, Five Ideal

Minimum: 1 chapter PDF set + 1 chapter test series + 1 sample recorded class. Ideal: add 1 bundled course package and 1 scheduled live class series. Pricing tiers: entry-level standalone content (₹99–999), mid-tier bundles (₹2,000–15,000), premium live programs (₹15,000+). This three-tier architecture serves every student budget level and creates discovery surfaces across price ranges.

The honest constraint here is that producing five content formats sounds operationally expensive — and on a stitched SaaS stack, it is. Different tools for video, PDFs, tests, live classes, bundles. Different access controls. Different analytics. On an educator OS, all five formats live under one profile, share one set of access controls, and surface in one analytics dashboard. The operational tax that historically prevented small educators from competing on catalog breadth simply does not exist anymore.

Publish what you have, even if it is not perfect. The catalog improves through engagement signals, not through pre-launch perfection. Educators who ship in day three and iterate based on what students actually buy outperform educators who polish in private for three months and ship a "complete" academy that turns out to be slightly off-target. Multiple SaaS tools means each tool stores its own version of the student record, and the educator's team becomes the integration layer. One login means student identity, payments, content access, communication, and analytics all share the same data and trigger each other natively. The first scales infinitely; the second breaks at every growth threshold.
