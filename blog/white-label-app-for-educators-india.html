    What is a white label LMS?

    A white label LMS is a learning management system that one company builds and maintains, which an educator rents and runs under their own brand. Students see your academy's name, logo, colours and domain on the website, checkout and app, never the vendor's. You get your own online academy without hiring developers.

    That is the white label LMS meaning in one paragraph. LMS stands for learning management system: the software that hosts your courses, plays your videos, runs your tests, takes payments and tracks your students. "White label" comes from retail, where one maker's product is sold under other companies' labels. In software it means one codebase, many brands.

    A white label learning management system for an Indian educator usually covers five parts:

    • An academy website on your own domain, with course pages and student login.
    • Checkout with UPI and cards, under your academy's name.
    • Learning tools: video player, notes, test series, live classes and certificates.
    • A branded mobile app for Android, and often iOS, which is the white label LMS app.
    • A panel where you change brand, courses and prices yourself.

    It does not mean you own the code. You rent the white label LMS software; you own your brand, content and student data. If the domain question matters most to you, our guide to an LMS with a custom domain for coaching covers that part step by step.

    What is a white label app for teachers?

    A white label app for teachers is an app built on a platform's shared software that is rebranded with your academy's name, icon, colours and content, and published under your own developer account. Students download it by your name and never need to know which platform runs it. You rent the software; the brand, the store listing and the students are yours.

    In a white label LMS the app is one part of the academy; some vendors sell only the app. Either way, it brings three benefits.

    • Your brand on the home screen. The icon, the name under it and the splash screen all say your academy.
    • No app project. No developers to hire; the platform maintains the software for every academy at once.
    • One system behind it. When the app is part of an LMS, the courses, tests and payments that run your website also run the app.

    What decides your real control is whose developer account the app sits in. If you are comparing LMS platforms mainly on their mobile app, shared app versus your own app versus a mobile website, our guide to choosing an LMS with a mobile app has a 12-point checklist for that decision.

    White label LMS vs white label app vs custom build

    A white label LMS gives you the whole academy under your brand (website, checkout, learning tools and app), a white label app vendor gives you mainly a branded app, and a custom build gives you software made only for you that you pay to build and keep updated. All three can put your name in front of students; they differ in how much you get and who carries the upkeep.

    What should white label include?

    A white label LMS or app should include six things: your own domain, your own brand, no platform branding shown to students, your own app listing, an export of your data, and checkout under your academy's name. If any one is missing, the vendor's name still sits between you and your students.

    1. Your domain. Students visit youracademy.in, not a page on the vendor's website. A free subdomain is fine to start; connecting your own domain should be possible on the plan you buy.
    2. Your brand. Your logo, colours, favicon and academy name on every page, email and certificate, changed by you from a panel.
    3. No platform branding to students. No "powered by" badge in the footer, the login screen or the app's splash screen. Some platforms remove the badge only on a higher plan, so ask which plan.
    4. Your own app listing. A separate app with your icon, published under your own Google Play and Apple developer accounts.
    5. Your data export. Students, purchases and content in a usable format, and a clear window to take them if you stop.
    6. Checkout under your name. Students pay on your academy's pages, and the course they buy says your academy, not the vendor's storefront.

    This is what full control looks like in practice. It is the same idea as an online store on your own domain instead of a stall in someone's marketplace: the customer relationship stays with you.

    Whose developer account is the app published under?

    Ask this first: a white label app should be published under your own Google Play and Apple developer accounts, because the account holder is the name stores show as the developer and the one who controls the listing. If the app sits in a vendor's account, the brand on the icon is yours but the listing is not.

    Apple is explicit about this. If you enrol in the Apple Developer Program as an individual, your personal legal name is listed as the seller on the App Store; if you enrol as an organisation, the legal entity name appears as the seller.3 Apple's App Review Guidelines also push white label apps towards the educator's own account. Guideline 4.2.6 reads:

    "Apps created from a commercialized template or app generation service will be rejected unless they are submitted directly by the provider of the app's content."

    Apple App Review Guidelines 4.2.6, checked 6 Nov 2026[4]

    For an academy, "the provider of the app's content" is you. Not every vendor works this way. Learnyst's pricing page, for example, says: "Apps will be available in Google Play store & Apple App store under Learnyst's Developer Account" (checked 6 Nov 2026).5 That is a legitimate model, but it means the publisher name and the listing stay with the vendor if you move.

    When educators come to us from an agency-built app, the first thing we ask is which Play Console account the app sits in, and the answer is not always clear to them. Find out before you sign, and get the answer in writing.

    Question educators ask

    Can I move my app from a vendor's account to my own later?

    Sometimes, but it is not automatic. Google Play and Apple both have app transfer processes, and they need the current account holder to start them. If the vendor will not, you may have to publish a new app under your own account and ask students to install it again. That is why the account question belongs before you choose, not after.

    Red flags: when white label is not really white label

    The red flags in a white label LMS or app are platform branding on student pages, one shared app for many academies, an app listed under the vendor's developer account, and no data export. Each can look minor in a sales chat and becomes expensive the day you want to grow or move.

    Use this checklist on every trial account or proposal, including ours:

    • Platform branding on student pages. Open a course page, the login screen and a certificate as a student would. If the vendor's logo or a "powered by" line appears, ask whether your plan removes it. Learnyst's pricing page, for example, lists "Remove 'Powered by Learnyst'" as a feature of its Premium plan (checked 6 Nov 2026).5
    • A shared app. If students must install the platform's app and then search for your academy inside it, you have a marketplace listing, not a white label app.
    • A listing under the vendor's developer account. The store shows the account holder as the developer. Ask whose Play Console and Apple account will publish the app, and get it in writing.
    • No data export. If the contract does not say how you export students, purchases and content, and for how long after you stop, assume you cannot.
    • Checkout in the vendor's name. If students are sent to a vendor storefront to pay, your brand drops out at the most important moment.
    • A promised store approval date. Google and Apple review every new app, so nobody can guarantee approval or a launch date.

    One mistake sits on the educator's side: promising students a launch date. Store review sets the timing, so announce the app once it is live in the store, and line up your Play testers early if Google asks for them.

    What to ask before you choose a white label LMS or app

    Before you choose a white label LMS or app provider, ask seven questions: whose developer account, can you change it yourself, can you export your data, is iOS included, who handles store review, what it costs every year, and what happens if you leave. Ask every provider the same seven, including us.

    1. Whose developer account? Yours is best; the listing and developer name stay with you.
    2. Can you change it yourself? Logo, colours, courses and prices should change from a panel, not by ticket.
    3. Can you export your data? Students, purchases and content; ask in which format and for how long after you stop.
    4. Is iOS included? It is often on a higher plan. Ask which plan, at what price.
    5. Who handles store review? Someone must submit builds, answer rejections and keep up with store policy changes.
    6. What does it cost every year? Plan, fee per sale, store fees, renewal and GST.
    7. What if you leave? What happens to the app, the listing and your data, and how long you have.

    Then run the red-flag checklist below on a trial account, as a student would see it. Answers in a sales chat are useful; what students actually see is proof.

    How to get a white label LMS and app for your academy: 7 steps

    Decide what the academy and app must do, choose a provider with the seven questions and the red flags, set your brand and connect your domain, open your own developer accounts, line up testers if Google asks for them, publish on your own account, then pass store review and share the link. Each step is short; the waiting is mostly on the store side.

    1. Decide what the academy must doList what students will use it for, such as recorded courses, test series, live classes and notes, and check whether most of your students use Android or iPhone.
    2. Choose a provider with the seven questionsAsk each white label LMS or app provider whose developer account the app is published under, whether you can change it yourself, how data export works, which plan includes iOS, who handles store review, the yearly cost and what happens if you leave. Then check a trial account for the red flags.
    3. Set your brand and connect your domainAdd your academy name, logo, icon and colours in the provider's panel and connect a domain you own. On AllCoaching this is Own branding in the studio.
    4. Open your own developer accountsRegister a Google Play Console account (US$25 once) and, if you need iOS, enrol in the Apple Developer Program (US$99 a year). Choose personal or organisation; organisation accounts need a D-U-N-S Number.
    5. Line up testers if Google asks for themIf your Play account is personal and new, run a closed test with at least 12 testers who stay opted in for at least 14 days before you apply for production access.
    6. Publish on your own accountPublish the build yourself, or connect your Play Console and App Store Connect accounts so the provider can publish on your behalf with API access.
    7. Pass store review, then share the linkGoogle and Apple review the app on their own timeline. Once it is live, share the store link and your academy website on YouTube, Instagram and WhatsApp.

    In our experience, the steps that take longest are 4 and 5: an organisation account waiting on its D-U-N-S Number, or a new personal Play account without twelve testers ready. Start both early, while you are still uploading courses.

    Google Play and App Store: fees, testing and review

    Google Play charges a US$25 one-time registration fee and the Apple Developer Program costs US$99 a year; both are paid by whoever owns the developer account, and both stores review your app before students can install it. These rules are the same whichever platform builds your app.

    "There is a US$25 one-time registration fee."

    Google Play Console Help, checked 6 Nov 2026[1]

    The closed test for new personal Play accounts

    Google requires developers with personal Play Console accounts created after 13 November 2023 to run a closed test with at least 12 testers who have been opted in continuously for at least 14 days before they can apply for production access.2 For an educator, those twelve testers can be colleagues, a few loyal students or family members with Android phones. The rule is for personal accounts; organisation accounts instead have to give a D-U-N-S Number when they register.7

    Apple's annual fee and enrolment

    Apple states the Apple Developer Program annual fee as 99 USD, in local currency where available, and asks organisations for a D-U-N-S Number during enrolment.3 Because the fee is yearly, put the renewal date in your calendar so your iOS app stays available.

    Review takes the time it takes

    Both stores review new apps, and updates can be reviewed too. The timing is decided by Google and Apple, not by your platform. Plan your launch announcement for after the app is live, not on a fixed date.

    Do you need an Android app, an iOS app or both?

    Start with Android if most of your students use Android phones; add iOS when a real share of your students or their parents use iPhones. Look at your own students before deciding, not at national averages.

    Ask in your WhatsApp group which phone students use, or check the device report in your website analytics. A course for working professionals or NRI students can have a very different mix from a NEET batch in a tier-2 city. Your website works on every phone meanwhile.

    Glossary

    White label LMS
    A learning management system built and maintained by one company and run by an educator under their own brand, domain and app, with no vendor branding shown to students.
    White label app
    An app built on a platform's shared software and rebranded with an educator's own name, icon and colours, usually published under the educator's own developer account.
    Custom build
    Software made for one educator by an agency or developer, paid for as a project and then maintained at the educator's cost.
    Marketplace app
    One app, under the platform's brand, that lists many educators' courses side by side.
    Developer account
    The account with Google Play or Apple that publishes an app. Its holder is shown as the app's developer or seller and controls the store listing.
    Google Play Console
    Google's console for publishing and managing Android apps on Google Play. Registration costs US$25 once.
    App Store Connect
    Apple's tool for submitting and managing apps on the App Store, used with an Apple Developer Program membership.
    Closed test
    A Google Play testing track open only to invited testers. New personal Play accounts must run one with at least 12 testers for 14 days before production.
    Store review
    Google's and Apple's check of an app against their policies before it is published. Timing is decided by the store.
    D-U-N-S Number
    A unique business identifier from Dun & Bradstreet that Google and Apple ask organisations for when they register a developer account.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is a white label LMS?

    A white label LMS is a learning management system that one company builds and maintains, which an educator rents and runs under their own brand. Students see the academy's name, logo, colours and domain on the website, checkout and app, never the vendor's, and the educator does not need to hire developers.

    What is the difference between a white label LMS and a white label app?

    A white label LMS is the whole academy under your brand: website on your domain, checkout, courses, tests, live classes and usually an app. A white label app is only the mobile part, with your name and icon. Some vendors sell just the app, so ask whether the website and payments are included.

    What is a white label app for teachers?

    A white label app for teachers is an app built on a platform's ready-made software but branded with the teacher's own name, icon and colours, and published under the teacher's own Google Play and App Store developer accounts. Students install it as the teacher's app, while the platform maintains the software.

    Is a white label app the same as having my own app?

    On the student's phone, yes: it carries your name and icon. The difference is ownership of the software. You own your brand, content and student data, while the platform owns and maintains the code. Publishing under your own developer account keeps the store listing in your name.

    Whose name appears as the developer of a white label app?

    The name of the developer account the app is published under. If it is your own account, the store shows your name or your academy's; on the App Store, Apple lists your legal name or your organisation's legal name as the seller. If a vendor publishes it under its own account, the vendor's name appears.

    Why does Google ask for 12 testers before my app goes live?

    Google requires personal Play Console accounts created after 13 November 2023 to run a closed test with at least 12 testers who stay opted in for at least 14 days before applying for production access. Colleagues, loyal students or family members with Android phones can be your testers.

    Does AllCoaching include a white label app on every plan?

Monthly plans start at ₹3,999 a month with 0% commission, and yearly plans at ₹6,999 a year with a fee per sale; prices exclude 18% GST.

    Can AllCoaching publish the app for me?

    Yes. The app is published on your own Google Play and App Store developer accounts, either by you or by AllCoaching with API access you grant through the Play Console and App Store Connect plugins. You pay the store fees directly to Google and Apple.

    How long does app store approval take?

    Google and Apple decide the timing, and no provider can promise an approval date. If you have a new personal Play account, add at least 14 days for the closed test. Announce your app once it is live in the store.

    What happens to my app if I stop using the platform?

    The store listing stays in your developer account, but the app depends on the platform to work. On AllCoaching, if you stop, your academy goes offline at the end of the paid period and you get 30 days to export your content and data or come back; after that, uploaded videos are deleted.
