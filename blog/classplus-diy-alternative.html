    Classplus DIY is Classplus's self-serve sign-up for its coaching app and website.     Who does Classplus DIY suit?

    Classplus suits a coaching owner who wants an app delivered for them, a published one-year price, and is comfortable paying for the whole year up front. Its pages promise delivery in 5–6 days, unlimited students and a test module, and a demo is there for those who prefer to talk first.

    • An established offline institute that wants its existing students on an app quickly, and already knows it will use it for at least a year.
    • An owner who prefers a team to set things up rather than work through settings alone.
    • A two-year plan buyer: Classplus recommends the ₹39,999 two-year plan for "medium & large coaching centres".

    It suits less well if you want to start small and pay monthly, stop any month, put the academy on your own domain from day one, or know your total cost including any fee per sale before you commit.     Classplus DIY vs your own app: shared app, branded app and developer account

    "Your own app" can mean three different things: your course inside the platform's shared app, a branded app published on the platform's developer account, or your own app published on your own Google Play developer account. Only the last one stays yours if you move. Ask which one you are buying.

    1. A shared app

    Students download the platform's app and find your batch inside it. Classplus Lite works this way: it is listed on Google Play under Bunch Microtechnologies and described as a free teaching app.5 It is quick to start, but the listing, the reviews and the name students search for belong to the platform.

    2. A branded app on the platform's account

    The app carries your name and logo, but the Play Store listing sits in the platform's developer account. Students see your brand. If you leave, the listing usually cannot move with you, so students install a new app.

    3. Your own app on your own developer account

    The app is published on your own Google Play Console (and App Store account for iOS). The listing, its reviews and its download history stay in your account, and a later platform change can update the same listing instead of starting from zero.

    Classplus's FAQ says the app "will be registered in your Coaching’s name and will bear your Logo".3 None of the pages we checked says whose developer account the listing is published on, so ask: "Will the app be published on my own Google Play developer account, and can I keep the listing if I leave?" Our guide to choosing an LMS with a mobile app explains the developer-account question in more detail.

    What to check before choosing any self-serve coaching app

    Before you pay any self-serve coaching app, Classplus or anyone else, get written answers on eight things: the kind of app, the developer account, your domain, the fee per sale, GST and gateway charges, renewal, cancellation, and how you export your students and content. Each answer changes either what you own or what you pay.

    1. Own app or shared app? Will students download an app with your name, or find you inside the platform's app?
    2. Whose developer account? Is the listing on your own Google Play Console, or the platform's?
    3. Your own domain? Can the website run on youracademy.in, or only on a platform address?
    4. Fee per sale? Is any percentage taken from each student payment, on top of the plan price?
    5. GST and gateway charges? Is GST included in the listed price, and what does the payment gateway charge per transaction?
    6. Renewal and cancellation? What does the second year cost, and can you stop before the term ends?
    7. Data export? Can you download your student list, payments and content, and for how long after you stop?
    8. Switching? If you move later, what happens to the app listing and to students in an active batch?

    From the Classplus pages we checked, items 1 (an app in your coaching's name) and 6 (renewal at the purchase price; no early cancellation) are answered; items 2, 3, 4, 5 and 7 are not stated.     How to evaluate a self-serve app and switch without losing students: 7 steps

    Ask your current or shortlisted platform the eight questions in writing, export your student list, build the new academy in a free trial, run both side by side, send new admissions to the new academy, and close the old plan only after the last batch ends. Nobody loses access, and you never pay two platforms for long.

    1. Ask the eight questions in writingSend your current or shortlisted platform the questions on app type, developer account, domain, fee per sale, GST, renewal, cancellation and data export, and keep the replies.
    2. Export your student listDownload names, phone numbers, emails and batch details as a spreadsheet before you change anything, and note each batch's end date.
    3. Build the new academy in a free trialUpload one full course, connect your domain and set your brand, so students will see your name, not the platform's.
    4. Test checkout on a phoneBuy your own course by UPI on a basic Android phone on mobile data, and fix anything that would make a student give up.
    5. Run both side by sideKeep current students on the old app until their batch ends; nobody who has paid loses access.
    6. Send new admissions to the new academyChange the link in your YouTube description, Instagram bio and WhatsApp group so every new student enrols in your own academy.
    7. Close the old plan after the last batchWhen the last batch on the old app ends, or the paid term expires, export everything once more and stop renewal.

    If you are on a one-year Classplus plan, step 7 lines up with its expiry date, because the terms do not allow early cancellation. The full message templates for students and parents are in how to switch coaching apps without losing your students.

    Mistakes to avoid with any self-serve coaching app

    The common mistakes are taking "DIY" to mean you own everything, comparing only the headline price, not asking whose developer account the app uses, paying for a year without reading the cancellation terms, and having no export plan. Each takes five minutes to avoid before you pay.

    • "DIY" means I own it all. Self-serve describes how you sign up, not who owns the listing or the domain. Ask both.
    • Only the headline price. Add any fee per sale, GST, gateway charges and the second year's renewal.
    • No question about the developer account. It decides whether students reinstall when you move.
    • Not reading cancellation terms. A yearly plan you cannot cancel is a year's commitment, whatever happens to your batch.
    • No export plan. Download your student list every month, wherever you teach.

    Glossary

    DIY (do it yourself)
    A self-serve way to start: you sign up and set things up without first speaking to a sales team. It describes how you begin, not who owns the result.
    Self-serve platform
    A platform you can sign up for, configure and pay for on your own, with published prices and no mandatory sales call.
    Shared app
    One app published by the platform under its own name, in which many educators' batches and courses sit side by side.
    White-label app
    An app built by a platform but shown under the educator's name and logo. Whose developer account publishes it is a separate question.
    Developer account
    The Google Play Console or Apple App Store Connect account an app is published from. The account holder controls the listing, its reviews and its updates.
    Renewal charge
    What you pay to continue a plan after its first term ends. Classplus's FAQ says its renewal equals the purchase price.
    Fee per sale
    A percentage of each student payment that a platform takes, on top of or instead of a fixed plan price.
    Custom domain
    Your own web address, such as youracademy.in, connected to your academy website instead of a platform sub-address.
