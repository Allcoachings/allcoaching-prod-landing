    The short answer: decide what you want to own

    The best Unacademy alternative depends on what you want to own. If you want your own brand, app and student list, build your own academy. If you want a large audience and are happy teaching under a platform's name, staying can be the right call. Many independent educators do both.

    The rest of this guide explains each path, what is and is not published, and how to start your own academy without giving anything up first.

    Why educators look for an Unacademy alternative

    Educators who look for an Unacademy alternative are usually not complaining about the platform. They want a business that carries their own name. The reasons tend to fall into a few groups.

    What a large platform does well

    Unacademy calls itself "India's largest online learning platform".[1] A platform of that size can put a capable educator in front of more students than most could reach alone, with an app, payments and support already built. For an educator who wants to teach and nothing else, that is a fair and valuable offer.

    1. Wanting your own name to grow

    On a large platform, students often find the platform first and the educator second. Educators who want students to search for them by name, and follow them wherever they teach, start building their own academy.

    2. Wanting to set your own price

    Your own academy lets you price a UPSC answer-writing programme, a GATE mechanical test series or a CUET English batch the way you think is fair, and change it when you want.

    3. Wanting to reach your students directly

    When you launch something new, you want to tell the students who already trust you. On your own academy, the student list is yours and exportable.

    Question educators ask

    Can I be my own Unacademy?

    Yes, at your own scale. You can run live classes, recorded courses, test series and notes in an app with your name on it. What you will not have on day one is a large platform's audience, so plan how students will find you: your YouTube channel, Instagram, WhatsApp and search.

    4. Wanting one place for everything

    Educators who grow a following on YouTube often end up with classes on one app, notes in Drive links and fees on UPI screenshots. One academy login replaces that.

    How to start your own academy while you still teach on a platform

    You can start your own academy without leaving anything first. Build it on the side, with fresh material, and let it grow at its own pace, if your agreement allows.

    1. Read your agreementCheck exclusivity, non-compete and content-ownership clauses. If something is unclear, ask the platform in writing or a lawyer; this guide is not legal advice.
    2. Build your academyStart the 14-day free trial, connect your own domain, add your logo and colours, and set up your Android app (iOS app on Pro and Scale).
    3. Create one fresh productRecord a new course or build a test series for your academy rather than reusing classes made for the platform.
    4. Bring your followersShare your academy link on YouTube, Instagram and WhatsApp, and put it in every video description.
    5. Grow your own wayKeep both, or move fully once your academy earns enough. Pick a plan when the trial ends.

    Glossary

    Learning platform
    A large service where many educators teach students under the platform's brand, with its app, payments and audience.
    Own academy
    A teaching business under your own name, with your own website, app, prices and student list.
    White-label app
    An Android or iOS app that carries your name and logo, built on a platform's technology.
    Platform fee
    A percentage a platform keeps from each sale. AllCoaching's is 10%, 7.5% or 5% depending on the plan.
    Exclusivity clause
    A contract term that limits where else you may teach while, or after, you work with a company.
