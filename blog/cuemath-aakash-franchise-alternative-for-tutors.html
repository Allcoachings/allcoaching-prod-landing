    The short answer: stay, or build your own brand when your agreement allows

    A franchise tutor has two honest options: stay in the franchise while it works for you, or build a coaching brand of your own when your agreement allows. Which one fits depends on your contract, your local reputation and how much of the marketing you want to own.

    If you are mid-agreement, the useful work now is preparation: read the agreement, note the dates, and plan the academy you will open when you are free to.

    Question educators ask

    Can I start my own brand while I still run a franchise centre?

    It depends entirely on your agreement. Many agreements have clauses on exclusivity, non-compete and territory that apply during the agreement and sometimes after it. Read them first, and ask a lawyer who handles franchise agreements if anything is unclear. Building your academy in private and launching later is different from selling to students now.

    What changes with your own brand

    With your own academy, families see your name, you set the fees and batches, and the student list is yours. You also take on what the brand did for you, mainly marketing. That trade is the real decision.

    What to read in your franchise agreement first

    Before you plan anything, read your franchise agreement end to end. These are the clauses that usually decide what you can do and when. We cannot interpret your contract; if any clause is unclear, ask a lawyer.

    1. Term, renewal and exit notice: when the agreement ends, whether it renews on its own, and how much notice you must give.
    2. Non-compete and exclusivity: whether you may teach or run a coaching business outside the franchise, during and after the agreement.
    3. Territory: any area where you may not operate, and for how long.
    4. Students and leads: who owns the student list, enquiries and contact details, and whether you may contact them later.
    5. Material and name: who owns the curriculum, workbooks, tests and recordings, and what must be returned or stop being used.
    6. Money on exit: any fees, deposits or dues that apply when you leave.

    Write down the dates and the answers. They tell you whether you can launch now, at renewal, or after a waiting period.

    Glossary

    Franchise
    An arrangement where a business lets you operate under its brand and system in return for fees or a share of revenue, on terms set by a written agreement.
    Non-compete clause
    A clause in an agreement that limits running a competing business, sometimes within an area or for a period after the agreement ends.
    Territory clause
    A clause that defines the area where a franchisee may, or may not, operate.
    Own-brand academy
    A coaching business under your own name, with your own website, app, fees and student list.
    Platform fee
    A percentage a platform keeps from each sale. AllCoaching's is 0% on Growth and Pro and 10% on Starter, charged only when you sell.
