    You can sell online courses without a monthly subscription in two ways: a pay-per-sale platform that takes a percentage of every sale and charges no fixed fee, or one annual plan with a smaller fee on sales (or none). Pay-per-sale usually costs least while sales are small; an annual plan costs less once yearly sales grow, from about ₹5 lakh in the examples below.

    Most educators meet the monthly bill at the worst moment. You teach SSC maths in Hindi on YouTube, or spoken English on Instagram, and decide to sell your first course. The platform you like bills you every month, in the months you sell well and in the months you sell nothing, such as the weeks after an exam when students disappear.

    This guide compares the three ways to pay for a course platform: a monthly subscription, pay per sale and an annual plan. It works out what each costs from no sales to ₹20 lakh a year, using published prices, and shows where each one wins. All cost figures are illustrative and before GST unless we say otherwise.

    Key takeaways

    • There are two ways to avoid a monthly bill: pay per sale (a percentage of each sale, no fixed fee) or one annual plan with a smaller fee on sales, or 0%.
    • Pay per sale costs least at small sales. With 10% on every sale, ₹1 lakh of sales costs ₹10,000 a year, before add-ons such as your own app or website.
    • An annual plan costs less as sales grow. In our worked table, an annual plan with a lower fee starts to cost less than a 10% commission at about ₹5 lakh of yearly sales.
    • AllCoaching plan breakevens (before GST): Starter costs least under ₹2.4 lakh, Growth from ₹2.4 to ₹4.8 lakh, Pro from ₹4.8 to ₹15 lakh, and Scale (0%) above ₹15 lakh.
    • Compare yearly totals, not headline prices. Add GST, gateway charges and every add-on you need, such as a branded app, website or custom domain.
    • Some 0% plans cost less than Scale. Learnyst Professional billed annually works out to ₹97,188 a year plus taxes, lower than Scale's ₹99,999, so compare what each includes.

    Can you sell online courses without a monthly subscription?

    Yes. A course platform charges you in one of three ways: a fixed monthly subscription, a percentage of every sale with no fixed fee (pay per sale), or one annual plan, usually with a smaller percentage on sales. Only the first one sends you a bill every month.

    The search "course platform without monthly fee" usually comes from one worry: paying a bill before you earn. Pay per sale removes that worry completely. An annual plan removes the monthly bill, but you pay once for the year, so it suits an educator who expects to sell steadily.

    Why a monthly subscription is hard on a new academy

    A monthly subscription is hard on a new academy because the bill is fixed while teaching income is not. Your sales move with exam calendars, admission seasons and your own launch dates, but the bill arrives on the same date every month.

    Teaching income in India is seasonal. A NEET or JEE educator sells most batches before the exam year starts; a board-exam educator is busy from October to February; a CUET or state PSC educator follows the notification. Between those peaks, many academies sell very little.

    A monthly plan also adds twelve renewal moments a year. That pressure pushes many educators to rush a launch or cut a price to cover the next bill, which is the opposite of what a new academy needs.

    When a monthly subscription is the right choice

    A monthly plan is not wrong in itself. It is the right choice when you want to test a platform for a month or two before committing, or when your sales are high and steady enough that a 0% fee saves more than the monthly bill costs.

    Card auto-debits for recurring bills in India run on e-mandates. In June 2022 the Reserve Bank of India raised the limit for recurring card payments that can run without a fresh one-time password from ₹5,000 to ₹15,000 per transaction.[1] If a monthly bill is above that limit, expect to approve the payment yourself, and check the RBI's current e-mandate rules before you rely on auto-debit.

    Question educators ask

    Is an annual plan just a subscription paid once a year?

    Yes, and that is the honest way to describe it. An annual plan is a yearly subscription: you pay once, for twelve months, and renew once a year. The difference from a monthly plan is one bill and one renewal decision instead of twelve, and a known cost for the year on day one.

    Pay-per-sale course platforms in India: when commission-only costs less

    A pay-per-sale course platform charges no fixed fee and keeps a percentage of every sale instead. It costs least when your sales are small, because a month with no sales costs nothing and the fee only grows when your income does.

    Two Indian examples, from their official pricing pages checked on 24 October 2026:

    • Exly Starter shows its annual upfront fee as "FREE" with a 10% commission on sales. Its add-ons are priced separately: a mobile app (iOS and Android) from ₹60,000 a year, a custom website from ₹60,000 a year and white-labelled branding from ₹20,000 a year. Source: Exly pricing.
    • TagMango Basic has no fixed fee and a 10% commission that includes the Indian payment gateway, and it is capped at 200 students. A custom domain needs its Freedom Setup at ₹24,999 a year on top of a plan, and all TagMango prices are plus GST. Source: TagMango pricing.

    So pay per sale is a good way to test an idea. If you are selling your first ₹50,000 of courses, a 10% commission costs ₹5,000, less than any annual plan.

    Where pay per sale stops being the low-cost choice

    Two things change as you grow. First, the commission grows with every sale: at ₹10 lakh of yearly sales, 10% is ₹1 lakh a year. Second, the things that make an academy feel like your own, such as your own app, your own website or your own domain, are often priced as add-ons on pay-per-sale plans. Add them and the "no fixed fee" plan has a fixed fee after all.

    Pay per sale fits

    Testing one course. Uncertain demand. Sales under about ₹2–3 lakh a year. You do not need your own app or domain yet.

    Look again when

    Sales pass about ₹5 lakh a year. You need your own app, website or domain as paid add-ons. A student cap limits your batch size.

    Costs that stay with any course platform

    Removing the monthly bill does not remove every cost. Payment gateway charges, GST, add-ons, marketing and your own time stay with you on any platform, so add them before you compare.

    Payment gateway charges

    Every UPI or card payment passes through a payment gateway. Razorpay's pricing page lists a 2% flat standard fee per transaction and notes 0% MDR on UPI for most transactions, so what you pay depends on how your students pay.[2] Some course platforms include gateway charges in their fee and some add them on top, so ask yours. Our guide to accepting UPI payments for online courses explains checkout in more detail.

    GST

    Most plan prices in this guide exclude GST, and Indian coaching services are generally taxed at 18% once you are registered. Add GST to every plan price before you compare, and read our guide on GST for coaching institutes for when registration applies.

    Add-ons

    List every feature you need and check whether it is included or extra: your own Android or iOS app, a website on your own domain, Zoom, branding and extra teacher logins. On some platforms these add-ons cost more each year than the plan itself.

    Marketing and your time

    No pricing model brings students on its own. You will still teach on YouTube, post on Instagram, answer on WhatsApp and perhaps run ads. Our guides to digital marketing for coaching institutes and Meta ads for coaching institutes cover both free and paid ways to find students.

    The market is large enough for every model to exist. IBEF puts India's edtech market at ₹64,875 crore (US$7.5 billion), projected to reach ₹2,50,850 crore by 2030–31.[3]

    Mistakes educators make when avoiding the monthly fee

    Glossary

    Monthly subscription
    A pricing model where a platform charges a fixed bill every month, whether or not you sell, sometimes with a fee on sales as well.
    Pay per sale
    A pricing model with no fixed fee where the platform keeps a percentage of every sale you make. Also called commission-only pricing.
    Annual plan
    A yearly subscription paid once for twelve months, usually lower than the same plan billed monthly, and often with a smaller fee on sales.
    Platform fee
    The percentage of each sale a course platform keeps for its service, such as 10%, 7.5%, 5% or 0% on AllCoaching depending on the plan.
    Breakeven
    The sales level at which two plans cost the same in a year. Above it, the plan with the lower percentage costs less.
    Payment gateway
    The service that processes UPI, card and netbanking payments and sends the money to the seller, for a per-transaction charge.
    E-mandate
    A standing instruction that lets a merchant charge your card or UPI account automatically for recurring payments, under RBI rules.
    Add-on
    A feature sold separately from a plan, such as a branded app, custom website, custom domain or extra integrations.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I sell online courses without paying a monthly subscription?

    Yes. Use a pay-per-sale platform that takes a percentage of each sale with no fixed fee, or one annual plan with a smaller fee on sales or none. Pay per sale usually costs least at small sales; an annual plan costs less as sales grow.

    Which is better for a new educator, pay per sale or an annual plan?

    If you expect under about ₹1 lakh of sales in your first year, pay per sale usually costs least on platform cost alone. If you need your own website, domain and app from the start, check what those add-ons cost; a low-priced annual plan that includes them can cost less overall.

    At what sales level does an annual plan cost less than a 10% commission?

    In our illustrative table, an annual plan with a 5% fee and a ₹24,999 price matches a 10% commission at ₹5 lakh of yearly sales and costs less above it, before GST and add-ons.

    Is an annual plan a subscription?

    Yes. An annual plan is a yearly subscription that you pay once and renew once a year. It replaces twelve monthly bills with one and fixes your platform cost for the year.

    Is there a zero commission course platform in India?
