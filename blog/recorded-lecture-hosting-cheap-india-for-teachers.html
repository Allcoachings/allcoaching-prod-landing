    Most teachers searching for "cheap video hosting" compare storage prices. Storage is the small cost. The cost that grows is bandwidth: every time a student streams a lecture, the data travels to their phone, and most hosting providers bill for that by the gigabyte. A library of fifty lectures can cost almost nothing to store and still produce a large bill in the month a batch actually watches it.

    This guide explains the three parts of a hosting bill, compares the routes Indian teachers use, lists what good lecture hosting needs, and shows what AllCoaching includes in each plan.

    What does recorded lecture hosting actually cost?

    A hosting bill has three parts: storage, encoding and delivery. Storage and encoding are small; delivery (bandwidth) is the large, recurring part.

    CostWhat it isHow it growsSize
    StorageKeeping the video filesWith library sizeSmall
    EncodingMaking every quality for every deviceOnce per uploadModest
    Delivery (bandwidth)Streaming to studentsWith every viewLarge

    Public price lists show the gap. Bunny.net, a popular video and CDN provider, lists storage from $0.01 per GB a month per region, while delivery is billed per GB on top: from $0.005 per GB on its volume network, and $0.03 per GB for Asia on its standard network.[1] A lecture is stored once but delivered every time a student watches it, so a popular course can cost far more to deliver than to store.

    Real hosting also needs encoding into several qualities and a content delivery network (CDN) so playback starts fast across India.[3] Raw storage includes neither. The technical side is covered in secure video hosting for educational content.

    YouTube unlisted vs Vimeo vs AWS vs Bunny: which costs least?

    Question educators ask

    I found a hosting plan for a few hundred rupees a month. Is that not cheap enough?

    It is cheap until students watch more than its bandwidth allowance. After that you pay per GB, and one popular launch can multiply the bill. You still have to add a player, logins and access control yourself. On a plan with hosting included there is no per-view bill, and if your library outgrows the allowance you move up a plan.

    Why does DIY hosting cost more than the price you see?

    Because a storage price leaves out the parts that grow: bandwidth, a player, access control and your own time.

    • Bandwidth that grows with views: the more your lectures are watched, the bigger the monthly bill.
    • Encoding and a player: you convert videos into several qualities and embed a working player, or pay for one.
    • Access control and student accounts: to keep paid lectures paid, you need logins and gating.
    • Maintenance: API keys, updates and broken embeds take hours you could spend teaching.

    What does good lecture hosting need?

    Good lecture hosting is storage plus fast delivery, adaptive playback, a player, phone-first viewing and access control, at a cost you know in advance.

    Glossary

    Recorded lecture hosting
    Storing and delivering an educator's recorded video lectures so students can stream them online, including delivery, a player and access control.
    Bandwidth (delivery cost)
    The data sent each time a student streams a video. Most providers bill it per GB, so it grows with views.
    Content delivery network (CDN)
    Servers spread across regions that keep copies of a video close to viewers so playback starts fast.
    Adaptive bitrate streaming
    Delivery that switches video quality to match each viewer's connection to reduce buffering.
    Hosting included in the plan
    Video storage and streaming priced into a teaching platform's plan instead of billed separately by usage.
    Access-controlled streaming
    Playing video only for logged-in students who have paid, instead of through an open link.
    Zero commission
    A plan where the platform takes no share of your course sales; on AllCoaching, every monthly plan.

    Is video hosting expensive because of storage or bandwidth?

    Bandwidth. Storage is cheap and paid once, but delivery is billed per GB every time a student watches, so a small library watched by many students can cost more than a large library watched by few.
