    The best platform to sell online courses in India: the short answer

    There is no single best platform to sell online courses in India; there are four kinds of platform, and the right one depends on who you want to own the student. A marketplace finds students for you but keeps them, a course-selling app gives you a storefront inside its system, an own-academy LMS puts your brand, domain and app first, and a DIY stack gives you everything to build and maintain yourself.

    "Where should I sell my course?" is really three questions: who will find my students, how much of each sale will I keep, and what happens to my students if I leave. Each type answers them differently.

    • No audience yet, testing a topic: a marketplace is a fair place to start.
    • You have a YouTube channel, a WhatsApp group or a batch of students: sell from your own academy, where the students stay yours.
    • A coaching institute with teachers and test series: an own-academy LMS with team seats and your own app.
    • A developer on hand and time to maintain it: a DIY stack can work.

    The 4 types of online course platforms in India

    Online course platforms in India fall into four types: marketplaces, course-selling apps, own-academy LMS platforms and DIY stacks. They differ less in features than in who the student belongs to, whose name the student sees, and how the money reaches you.

    1. Marketplaces (Udemy-style)

    A marketplace is a large catalogue where students browse many teachers' courses. You upload, the marketplace brings traffic, runs the checkout and pays you a revenue share. The student's account, reviews and usually the pricing rules belong to the marketplace, so if you leave, your students stay behind.

    2. Course-selling apps and storefronts (Classplus, Graphy, Exly-type)

    These platforms give you a store or an app inside their system, a checkout, and tools to sell courses, tests or sessions. The student is yours to market to, but your own branded app, your own domain or a white-label setup is often a higher tier or a paid add-on. Exly, for example, lists a mobile app add-on starting at ₹60,000 a year and a custom website add-on starting at ₹60,000 a year on its pricing page.6

    3. Own-academy LMS (AllCoaching, Learnyst, Edmingle)

    An own-academy LMS builds an academy that carries your name: a website on your domain, your logo and colours, courses, test series and live classes, and on many plans an app in your name. The student enrols with your academy. Payment usually reaches you through a gateway account in your name, and your student list can be exported. They differ mostly in price, fee per sale and which plan includes the app.

    4. DIY stacks (WordPress + an LMS plugin, Teachable, Thinkific)

    A DIY stack means you assemble the parts: a WordPress site, hosting, an LMS plugin such as Tutor LMS, a payment gateway, a video host, and an app built separately if you want one. Global course builders like Teachable and Thinkific do much of the assembly for you but are priced in US dollars. Full control comes with full responsibility: if a plugin update breaks checkout on launch night, you fix it.

    Question educators ask

    Is Udemy or my own website better for selling courses in India?

    Udemy is better for finding your first students without an audience, because it brings the traffic. Your own website or academy is better once students already come to you from YouTube, Instagram or WhatsApp, because they enrol with you, you set the price, and you can sell to them again.

    Which type of platform fits which educator

    Start from where your students come from today: with no audience, a marketplace can find your first buyers; with any audience of your own, sell from your own academy so the students stay with you. Here is how that plays out for four common educators.

    A beginner with a YouTube audience

    Say you teach spoken English or Excel on YouTube with a few thousand subscribers. Your viewers already know your face, so they do not need a marketplace to find you; they need a link in the description that opens your course. An own academy with a small fixed fee fits, and at low sales a yearly plan with a fee per sale usually costs less than a monthly one. Our guide on how to sell courses online in India walks through the first ₹10,000.

    An exam coach with 500 students

    A NEET, SSC or banking coach with 500 students needs test series with rank, a live classroom, recordings and an app students open every day. At this size a fee per sale adds up quickly, so a 0% plan usually leaves more, and the branded app should be in the plan you can afford.

    A skill educator selling cohorts and workshops

    Yoga, music, vastu or digital marketing educators often sell a mix of live cohorts, recorded courses and one-to-one sessions. A storefront can suit sessions and bookings; once the recorded course is the main product, an own academy keeps it under your domain.

    A coaching institute

    An institute with several teachers needs team logins, an iOS app for some students, bigger video storage and a website parents trust. Compare the own-academy plan that includes iOS, not just the starting price. If you are leaving an existing app, our Classplus alternative guide covers switching without losing students.

    When a marketplace really is the right choice

    A marketplace is the honest answer when you have no audience and want to test whether anyone will pay for a topic, and it can run alongside your own academy for discovery. The marketplace owns the student and sets the share, so plan to bring your best students to your own academy over time. Our Udemy alternative guide explains how to keep both.

    How to choose a platform and start selling: 7 steps

    Know where your students come from, estimate a year of sales, pick the type of platform, compare two or three on total cost, check ownership, test one real course in a free trial, then launch to the audience you already have. The whole decision fits in a week.

    1. Write down where your students come fromList your audience today: YouTube subscribers, Instagram followers, a WhatsApp group, a classroom batch or none yet. This decides whether you need a platform that finds students or one that keeps them.
    2. Estimate a year of salesMultiply a realistic number of students by your course price, for example 100 students at ₹1,999. Use it only to compare costs; it is not a target.
    3. Pick the type of platformNo audience: start on a marketplace. Your own audience: an own-academy LMS. Sessions and workshops: a course-selling storefront can fit. A developer on hand: a DIY stack.
    4. Compare two or three on total costFor each, add the yearly price, the fee per sale at your estimated sales, the gateway fee, GST and any add-on such as an app or a custom domain.
    5. Check who owns whatConfirm you can use your own domain, publish an app in your name, set your own prices and export your students and content.
    6. Test one real course in a free trialUpload one module, connect UPI checkout, and buy it yourself on a phone on mobile data. Fix anything that would make a student give up.
    7. Launch to the audience you haveShare the course link with your YouTube, Instagram and WhatsApp audience, then review sales and costs after 90 days and adjust the plan.

    Step 6 is where most problems show up: buy your own course by UPI on a basic Android phone on mobile data. If you would not finish that journey, neither will your students.

    Common mistakes when choosing where to sell online courses

    The common mistakes are choosing by the headline price, ignoring the fee per sale, letting students live only on someone else's app, having no export plan, and paying in dollars for students who pay in rupees. Each one is easy to avoid before you start and hard to fix later.

    • Choosing by headline price. Work out the total for a year at your expected sales, as in the table above.
    • Ignoring the fee per sale. 10% of ₹10 lakh is ₹1 lakh a year. Write the fee next to every price you compare.
    • Students live on their app. Prefer your own domain and app, so a later move does not mean every student reinstalling.
    • No export plan. Ask how you export students and content before you pay, not when you leave.
    • Dollar plan, rupee students. A USD plan moves with the exchange rate; you still need UPI checkout and GST invoices.

    Glossary

    Revenue share
    The part of each sale a platform pays to the educator, with the platform keeping the rest. Common on marketplaces, where the platform sets the split.
    Commission (fee per sale)
    A percentage of each sale that the platform takes, on top of or instead of a fixed subscription price.
    Payment gateway
    The service that takes a student's UPI, card or netbanking payment and passes the money to the seller's account, for a fee per transaction. Razorpay is one example.
    MDR
    Merchant discount rate: the fee a merchant pays on each card or digital payment. Gateways usually show it as a percentage of the transaction.
    Settlement
    The transfer of collected payments, minus fees, from the payment gateway to the seller's bank account.
    White-label
    A product built by one company but shown under another's name, such as an app published with the educator's logo and name instead of the platform's.
    LMS
    Learning management system: software to host courses, tests and live classes, enrol students and track their progress.
    Course marketplace
    A platform where many teachers list courses in one catalogue and students buy from the platform, which brings traffic and keeps the student relationship.
    Own academy
    An online academy that runs on the educator's own brand and domain, often with its own app, where students enrol directly with the educator.

    What is the best platform to sell online courses in India?

    There is no single best platform. A marketplace suits a beginner with no audience, a course-selling app suits educators selling sessions and small products, an own-academy LMS suits educators who want their own brand, domain and app, and a DIY stack suits those with a developer.     Is Udemy good for Indian teachers?

    Udemy can be good for finding first students without an audience, because it brings the traffic. The trade-off is that Udemy owns the student relationship and sets the revenue share. Many educators use a marketplace to test a topic and then sell from their own academy.

    How much does it cost to sell online courses in India?

    It depends on the platform and your sales. Illustratively, at ₹2 lakh of sales a year, the lowest-cost plan on the rupee platforms we checked ranged from ₹20,000 to ₹1,07,988 before GST and gateway charges. Add a gateway fee of about 2% per payment on top.

    What is the difference between a marketplace and my own academy?

    On a marketplace, students buy from the platform's catalogue, the platform runs checkout and pays you a share, and the student account belongs to the platform. In your own academy, students enrol on your website or app, you set the price, the money reaches your account, and you can export your student list.

    Can I sell online courses on my own website in India?

    Yes. An own-academy LMS gives you a course website on your own domain with UPI and card checkout, or you can build one yourself with WordPress, an LMS plugin and a payment gateway. The first is faster to set up; the second gives full control but you maintain every part.
